# Time away from work

This page is for anybody who has to put time away from work into the system: a week of leave, a sick day, an afternoon at the doctor, a day the company has agreed to. It is also for whoever agrees or refuses those. It says how to record one, what each kind of absence does to a month, how half days and part days are counted, and what can still be changed afterwards.

Leave itself — how much somebody has, and how a day of it is valued — is on the page **Leave**. Public holidays are on **Public holidays**. This page is about the record of being away.

## Where this page is

The page is called **Absences**. It is named that in the trail across the top of the screen, after **Team time**. The first line on the page itself reads **Time away from work, and what it does to the month.**

If you look after the team:

1. Open **My time** from the sidebar.
2. Press the **…** button at the top of that page and choose **Everyone’s hours**. You are now on **Team time**.
3. In the row of links beside the month name, press **Absences**.

If you do not look after the team, nothing in the product links to this page. Ask for its web address and keep it as a bookmark; it is the address of your workspace followed by `/team-time/absences`. The page will open for you, and it will show your own absences and nobody else's.

What you see differs in three ways:

|                                           | You look after your own hours only | You look after the team |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| The list holds                            | your own absences                  | everybody's             |
| The button top right says                 | **Ask for time off**               | **Record absence**      |
| The **Reasons** button                    | not shown                          | shown                   |
| **Agree**, **Refuse**, **Delete absence** | not shown                          | shown                   |
| What a new absence is saved as            | **Asked for**                      | **Agreed**              |

If the page says **This is not yours to see**, with **There is no employment record for you here, so there is nothing to show. Ask whoever looks after the team to set one up.**, then nobody has set you up in the system yet. That is the whole of the problem, and the person who looks after the team can fix it.

## What the list shows

The page opens on the current month. Use the two arrow buttons beside the month name — **Previous month** and **Next month** — to move a month back or forward. There is no limit either way: you can step into any past or future month.

**Show absences for** narrows the list to one person. It starts on **Everyone**. If you only look after your own hours, the only name in it is yours.

An absence is listed in every month it touches. One running from 28 January to 3 February appears in January and in February. The list is ordered by first day away, latest at the top.

| Column         | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Person**     | Whose absence it is. A dash appears here for a moment while the list of people is still arriving.                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Reason**     | The kind of absence. Anything typed into **Note** follows it in grey.                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **When**       | One date, or the first and last date with a dash between them. The year is not shown, because the month is already named above the list. Underneath the wording is a grey note where the absence is not whole days: **Half day**, **Half day at each end** or **A set number of hours a day**. |
| **Hours away** | How much working time the absence covers, as hours and minutes. `38:30` means 38 hours and 30 minutes.                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **State**      | See below.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Action**     | The buttons for that row.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

The **When** column never says which half of the day, or how many hours. To see those, open the row with the pencil.

The **State** is one of these:

| On screen     | Colour            | What it means                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------- | ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Asked for** | amber             | Somebody has asked for this time off. It has not been agreed, and it counts for nothing at all — not in the month, not in the balance, not against leave. |
| **Agreed**    | green             | It counts. This is the only state that reaches any figure.                                                                                                |
| **Refused**   | red               | It was refused, with a reason kept alongside the record. It counts for nothing.                                                                           |
| **Withdrawn** | grey, crossed out | Nothing on this page produces this.                                                                                                                       |
| **Draft**     | grey              | Nothing on this page produces this.                                                                                                                       |

Where the month has been closed, the grey words **month closed — reopen it to change this** appear beside the state, and that row has no buttons left.

If the month has nothing in it the page says **No absences recorded for this month.** Nothing is wrong; step to another month, or clear **Show absences for**.

## Recording an absence

1. Press **Record absence**, top right. A dialog opens headed **Record an absence**.
2. **Person**. It starts on **Select**. Choose the person the absence belongs to. If you only look after your own hours, your own name is filled in already.
3. **Reason**. It also starts on **Select**. Choose the kind of absence. The list is explained further down this page.
4. **First day away** and **Last day away**. Both start on today's date, whatever month the list behind the dialog is showing. For a single day, set both to the same date. If you move the first day past the last, the last day moves with it.
5. **How much of each day**. Leave it on **Whole days** unless the person is away for only part of a day. The other two choices are explained in the next section.
6. **Note**, marked **Optional**. Anything typed here shows in grey after the reason in the list. It is not required and nothing is calculated from it.
7. Press **Record**.

The button stays grey until a person and a reason have both been chosen. After it works, the dialog closes, the list refreshes, and a green message says **Absence saved**.

If it does not work, the dialog stays open and a red message appears headed **That could not be saved**, with the reason beneath it. The commonest reason is a month that has already been handed in or closed — see [Once the month has been closed](#once-the-month-has-been-closed).

Two things worth knowing:

- **The dates always open on today.** Stepping the list back to March and then opening the dialog does not set the dates to March. If you are entering something for an earlier month, change both dates yourself.
- **Nothing checks for a second absence on the same day.** Recording the same week twice is accepted, and both rows will sit in the list. The month itself only ever counts a day once, but the list will read as though somebody was away twice.

## Asking for time off

If you do not look after the team, the button top right says **Ask for time off**. Press it and fill the dialog in exactly as above.

The dialog itself still reads **Record an absence**, and its button still reads **Record**, and the message afterwards still says **Absence saved**. None of that means the time off has been agreed. Look at the row that appears: it will say **Asked for**, in amber. Until somebody who looks after the team changes it to **Agreed**, the absence changes nothing anywhere.

Nothing tells anybody that you have asked. There is no email and no notice in the product. Say it to the person as well, and check the row later to see that it has turned green.

## The kinds of absence, and what each does to a month

Two questions decide what a reason does, and they are the two columns you see in the **Reasons** dialog:

- **Counts as worked** — whether the day still counts towards the hours for that month. Almost every reason does. Unpaid leave does not.
- **Off the leave account** — whether the time comes out of the person's leave for the year. Only annual leave does.

If the system was set up in the usual way, six reasons exist:

| Reason                  | Counts as worked | Off the leave account |
| ----------------------- | ---------------- | --------------------- |
| Annual leave            | Yes              | Yes                   |
| Sick leave              | Yes              | No                    |
| Time off in lieu        | Yes              | No                    |
| Care leave              | Yes              | No                    |
| Other justified absence | Yes              | No                    |
| Unpaid leave            | No               | No                    |

Time off in lieu is the one with an extra effect: the day is credited like any other paid absence, and the same hours are then taken off the person's running balance when the month is closed. That is what stops the hours being both credited and kept.

**Where two absences cover the same day**, the day is only counted once, and sick leave takes it. That is how a day taken ill in the middle of booked leave is charged to sickness and the leave stays. Care leave and other justified absence come next, then time off in lieu, and annual leave last of the paid reasons. A reason somebody adds by hand does not come with that ordering — it ranks alongside annual leave.

**Somebody who keeps no leave account.** Two of the arrangements here are self-invoicing, and those people keep no leave account. For them an annual-leave absence credits nothing and spends nothing: the day shows as a shortfall as though it had not been recorded. That is deliberate. If one of those colleagues is away, record it under a reason that does not come off the leave account.

## Whole days, half days, and a set number of hours

**How much of each day** has three settings.

**Whole days.** Every day between the first and the last counts as a whole scheduled day. Days the person does not work count as nothing at all. A week of leave for somebody who works four days a week costs four days, not five.

**Half day at each end.** Half days happen at the two ends of a stretch away, which is where they actually happen — somebody leaves at midday on the Friday, or comes back at midday on the Monday. Every day in between is a whole day.

- When the first and last day are the same, the setting reads **Half day** and one further box appears, **Which half**, with **Morning** and **Afternoon**.
- When it is a range, two boxes appear: **First day** and **Last day**, each with **Morning** and **Afternoon**.

A half is half of that person's scheduled day. Where the day has an odd number of minutes, the extra minute goes to the morning: on a day of 7:45 the morning is 3:53 and the afternoon 3:52. A morning and an afternoon on the same day therefore still add up to exactly one day.

**A set number of hours a day.** One further box appears, **Hours away each day**, with the example **e.g. 3:30** in it. The number you type applies to every day of the range. This is the setting for an afternoon at the doctor.

The hint under the box reads **Hours on each day of the range, written as 3:30, 3.5 or 3h. For minutes write 90m.** Those are the four ways it can be written:

| What you type  | What it means    |
| -------------- | ---------------- |
| `2:30`         | Two hours thirty |
| `2.5` or `2,5` | Two hours thirty |
| `3h`           | Three hours      |
| `90m`          | Ninety minutes   |

A bare number is read as hours. `90` means ninety hours, not ninety minutes, and the box will turn red and say **That is not a length of time. Try 3:30, 3.5, or 90m for ninety minutes.** Write `90m` when you mean minutes.

Whatever you type is capped at the length of the person's day. If they are scheduled 6:30 on a Friday and you record 8:00, the Friday counts 6:30.

## The Hours away figure

**Hours away** is how much working time the absence covers. It is worked out from the person's own working week, once, at the moment the absence is recorded, and then left alone.

Three consequences follow, and each of them looks like a fault the first time it is seen.

**An absence on days somebody does not work reads 0:00.** Booking a Friday off for somebody who does not work Fridays is accepted, shows as **Agreed**, and reads `0:00`. That is the right answer. Nothing was owed that day, so nothing was away and no leave was spent.

**Changing somebody's working week later does not change absences already recorded.** The figure was fixed against the week they had at the time. That is on purpose: recomputing it would quietly change how much leave somebody spent two years ago.

**Hours away does not know about public holidays.** A week of leave containing a public holiday still shows the whole week here, but the month pays for that day out of the holiday and only the rest comes off the person's leave. Anna books Monday to Friday and the Thursday is a public holiday; on a 7:42 day, **Hours away** reads 38:30 while only 30:48 is actually charged to her leave. Both figures are right for what they measure. To see what leave was really spent, read the leave figure on **My time — your own month**, not this column.

This screen counts in hours and minutes throughout. Leave elsewhere is counted in days, where a day means that person's own average scheduled day. How many days of leave somebody has left is on **My time — your own month**, and how that figure is worked out is on **Leave**. Neither is shown here.

## What an absence does to the month

The hours somebody is expected to work — shown as **To work** on their own month page — never go down. An absence that counts as worked is added to **Worked** instead, so the month comes out level. That keeps the hours handed to payroll equal to the hours the person is paid for.

The dialog says the same thing in one line: **Most reasons still count towards the hours for the month, so the month does not come out short. Days this person does not normally work are not counted.**

### A month worked through

Daniel is owed 7:42 a day, Monday to Friday. In this month there are 21 working days. One of them is a public holiday. He takes two days of annual leave, on days other than the holiday.

|                                   | Days                  | Hours      |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------- | ---------- |
| **To work**                       | 21 × 7:42             | **161:42** |
| At his desk                       | 18 × 7:42             | 138:36     |
| Credited by the public holiday    | 1 × 7:42              | 7:42       |
| Credited by his two days of leave | 2 × 7:42              | 15:24      |
| **Worked**                        | 138:36 + 7:42 + 15:24 | **161:42** |
| **Balance**                       | 161:42 − 161:42       | **0:00**   |

His leave account is charged the 15:24 — the two days of leave and nothing else. The public holiday costs him no leave, and neither would a sick day.

Had the public holiday fallen inside his two days of leave, the **Hours away** column would still have read 15:24, but only 7:42 would have come off his leave: the holiday takes its day first, and the absence draws on what is left.

Had those two days been unpaid leave, nothing would have been credited for them. **To work** would still read 161:42, **Worked** would read 146:18, and the balance would be 15:24 short.

### When the figures actually move

Recording or agreeing an absence does not change anybody's monthly figures at that moment. The month is worked out again the next time somebody opens it, brings it up to date, hands it in or closes it. For the current month and the one before, it also happens by itself about once an hour. If you agree somebody's leave and then look at their month and see nothing changed, nothing is broken — look again after opening the month.

## Agreeing and refusing

Only somebody who looks after the team sees these, and only on a row that says **Asked for**.

**To agree**, press the tick. It happens at once, with no confirmation step. The row turns green and says **Agreed**, a message appears saying **Agreed**, and the tick and the cross go away.

**To refuse**, press the cross. A dialog opens with the person's name in its heading — **Refuse Anna's time off** — and the line **This is kept with the record. Nobody is told automatically, so tell them yourself as well.** Type into **Why** — the example shown is _Two people are already away that week_ — and press **Refuse**. Something has to be written; the button stays grey while the box is empty. The row turns red and says **Refused**.

Two rules the screen does not state:

- **You cannot decide on your own absence**, even if you look after the team. Pressing the tick gives _Deciding on your own absence is not a decision. Ask someone else._ Somebody else has to do it.
- **Nobody is told anything.** No email, no notice in the product, at any point. Whoever looks after the team should open this page and clear the amber rows before each month is handed in, and a refusal has to be said to the person in words.

Every amber row is time that has not been counted. If a month is closed with somebody's leave still showing **Asked for**, the closed figures are short by exactly that much, and getting it back means reopening the month.

## Changing an absence

The pencil opens the same dialog, headed **Change this absence**, filled in from the row. The button at the bottom reads **Save changes**.

- **Person cannot be changed.** The box is greyed. An absence recorded against the wrong person has to be deleted and recorded again.
- Everything else can be changed while the month is still open.
- If you look after your own hours only, the pencil disappears once the absence has been agreed. Ask for the change to be made.
- The pencil is also gone on anything **Refused** or **Withdrawn**, and on any row in a closed month.

Changing the dates recalculates **Hours away** against the person's working week as it stands now.

## Deleting an absence

**Deleting cannot be undone.** There is no restore. If you delete the wrong row you have to record it again from memory, and it will be saved as a new record.

Only somebody who looks after the team can delete.

1. Press the bin on the row.
2. A dialog opens headed **Delete this absence?** It names the person and the hours, so for a two-day absence of Daniel's it reads: _15:24 stops counting towards Daniel's month, so the month will be short unless those hours are worked. Any leave this used goes back._
3. Read the hours figure in that sentence and check it is the row you meant.
4. Press **Delete absence**. It reads **Deleting…** while it works.

The row disappears and a green message says **Absence deleted**. If the absence came off somebody's leave, that leave is available again the next time the figures are worked out.

## Keeping the list of reasons

The **Reasons** button, beside the button at the top right, is only shown to somebody who looks after the team. It opens **Reasons for being away**, with the line **Two answers decide the arithmetic: whether the day still counts as worked, and whether it comes off the leave account.**

The table lists every reason, with **Counts as worked** and **Off the leave account** shown as **Yes** or **No**. A reason that has been retired is shown crossed out.

**To stop a reason being offered**, press **Retire** on its row. It disappears from the **Reason** box when somebody records an absence, and every absence already recorded under it stays readable and correctly named. **Bring back** puts it back.

**To add a reason**, fill in the two boxes at the bottom and press **Add**.

- **Code, e.g. URLAUB** — a short code. It is turned into capitals when it is saved.
- **Name people will read** — the name that appears in the lists. The same name is used in both English and German, so a reader in either language sees what you typed.
- **The day still counts as worked** — ticked to start with. Leave it ticked unless the day is genuinely unpaid.
- **The day comes off the leave account** — not ticked to start with. Tick it only for something that is genuinely annual leave.

Two warnings about this box:

- **The example code shown is already in use.** Annual leave is already set up under that code. Adding a reason with a code that only differs in capitals gives you a second reason that looks like annual leave but does not come off the leave account, because the box starts with that switch off. Give a new reason its own code, such as `TRAINING`.
- **Ticking the second box while unticking the first** creates a reason that spends somebody's leave and credits nothing towards their month. Nothing warns you about that combination.

**Deleting a reason** is refused as soon as anything has been recorded under it. The message says how many absences there are and ends: _Press Retire instead: it disappears from the list of reasons somebody can choose, and the existing ones stay readable._ That is the right answer — press **Retire**.

Press **Close** to leave the dialog.

## Once the month has been closed

A month stops being rebuilt as soon as it has been handed in. From that point nothing about an absence in it can be recorded, changed, agreed, refused or deleted, and anything written into it would never reach the figures.

Rows in a closed month carry the grey words **month closed — reopen it to change this** and have no buttons at all.

Trying anyway gives a message headed **That could not be saved**, reading: _May 2026 has been closed, so its records can no longer change. Ask whoever looks after the team to reopen it on Team time._ The word in the middle is **handed in**, **agreed** or **closed**, depending on how far the month has got. Reopening is done on **Team time**, needs a written reason, and is recorded. The page **The month, from open to closed** explains it.

Two details worth knowing:

- Closing a month freezes **every** absence that touches it, whatever its state. A refused request sitting in a closed month can no longer be deleted either.
- An absence that runs across a month end is frozen by whichever of the two months is closed first. Closing January freezes an absence running 28 January to 3 February, even though February is still open.

## Things that catch people out

- **Only green counts.** An amber **Asked for** row changes nothing anywhere. Clear the amber rows before a month is handed in.
- **The dialog opens on today**, whatever month you had stepped to. Check both dates before pressing **Record**.
- **Pressing Record does not agree anything** if you do not look after the team. It asks.
- **Nobody is notified**, ever, of a request, an agreement or a refusal.
- **People who have left are still in the lists.** The **Person** box and **Show absences for** both include former colleagues, with nothing to tell them apart. Read the name before you save.
- **`90` in the hours box means ninety hours.** Write `90m`.
- **`0:00` in Hours away is not a failure** — it means the days recorded are days that person does not work.
- **Hours away and leave spent can differ**, legitimately, when a public holiday falls inside the absence.
- **The figures do not move when you press Agree.** They move when the month is next opened or brought up to date.
- **Nothing stops two absences on the same day**, and nothing stops somebody being granted more leave than they have.

## If you get stuck

- Your own month, your figures and how much leave you have left: **My time — your own month**.
- What a day of leave is worth, and how the leave year runs: **Leave**.
- What a public holiday is worth, and who it is worth nothing to: **Public holidays**.
- Handing a month in, agreeing it, closing it, and getting one reopened: **The month, from open to closed**.
- Everybody's months at once, and reopening one: **Team time — everybody’s month**.
- Setting somebody's working week, their contract and their leave: **People and their terms**.
- A message you do not recognise, or a figure you cannot account for: **When something goes wrong**.

If none of those answers it, ask whoever looks after the team. A month that has already been closed can only be put right by them.
