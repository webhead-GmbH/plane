# Team time — everybody's month

This page is for whoever looks after the team. **Team time** is the one screen that shows everybody's month side by side, and the only place a month is agreed and then closed so its figures stop moving. This page says what the four figures at the top are counting, what each row of the table is telling you, which button appears when, what cannot be taken back, and what goes to the payroll accountant.

If you only need to look after your own hours, this screen is not for you. Read "My time — your own month" instead.

## Getting to the screen

There is no entry for this screen in the sidebar on the left. The way in is:

1. In the sidebar, open **My time**. That is your own month.
2. At the top right of that screen, press the button with three dots.
3. In the menu that opens, choose **Everyone's hours**.

You are now on Team time. The heading at the top of the window reads **Team time**.

**Everyone's hours** is only in that menu for people who have been set up as looking after the team. If it is not there, you have not been given that. Ask whoever set your account up; the setting is on the People screen and is described in "People and their terms".

If somebody who has not been given it reaches the address anyway, the row of links along the top still appears, but the body of the screen reads:

> **This is not yours to see**
> Everyone's hours are only readable by whoever looks after the team. Your own month is on My time.

There is no button to try again there, because nothing failed. If the screen instead reads **This could not be loaded**, something went wrong on the way; press **Try again**, and say so if it keeps happening.

## Which month you are looking at

The month is named at the top left — for example, October 2026. A chevron sits on either side of the name. The left one steps back a month, the right one steps forward. There is no "today" button.

**The screen opens on last month, not this one.** That is deliberate: last month is the month being handed in, agreed and closed. Your own **My time** screen opens on the current month, so the two screens do not agree about "now" when you have both open. Read the month name before you act on a row.

Neither chevron ever stops. You can step forward into months that have not happened and back to before anybody was employed. Those months look like months in which nobody recorded anything: every figure 0:00, and every row saying "The month is not over yet". Looking at them does no harm.

## The four figures at the top

Four cards sit above the table. Each one is the sum of the column beneath it and nothing else, so if you add up a column by hand you get the card.

Every length of time on this screen is hours and minutes with a colon between them. **161:42 means 161 hours and 42 minutes**, not 161.42 hours. A minus sign in front means somebody is behind.

### To work

What everybody's terms ask of them in this month, added up.

It comes from the working week recorded for each person on the People screen — how long each of their days should be — counted over the days of this month. Nothing takes hours off it. A public holiday does not reduce it. A day of paid leave does not reduce it.

**While a month is still running, this figure is only the days counted so far.** Open the screen on the 10th and it shows what was owed up to the 10th, not what the whole month owes. The whole-month figure is on the screen, but only in one place: rest the mouse pointer on a person's **To work** cell in the table and a small label appears reading, for example, "of 161:42 for the whole month". That label does not appear on a phone or a tablet, and it does not appear if you move around with the keyboard. Once the month has ended the two figures are the same and the label is gone.

Somebody whose terms do not expect set hours each day — the self-invoicing arrangements — adds 0:00 to this figure, always. That is not a fault. Nothing is owed by the day under those terms.

### Worked

What everybody's month came to, added up. For one person, one day, it is:

    hours on work items

- hours with no work item
- paid time off that counts as worked
- public holidays
  = Worked

**Paid time off and public holidays are added to Worked rather than taken off To work.** This surprises people, and nothing on this screen says it. It is done that way so that the hours figure handed to payroll is the number of hours the person is actually paid for. The Balance comes out the same either way.

So a week of annual leave shows up as a full week of hours in this column, and somebody who was away for a whole month can show a Worked figure equal to their To work figure and a Balance of exactly 0:00 — indistinguishable, on this screen, from somebody who worked every day. If you need to know how much of the figure was time off, the day-by-day breakdown is on that person's own **My time**, and the hours behind it are on **Hours in detail**.

What is _not_ in Worked:

- A timer that is still running. It has no length yet, so it counts nothing. It shows as a small timer mark beside the name instead.
- Absences that are still **Asked for**. Only absences shown as **Agreed** count towards anybody's month. See "Time away from work".
- A public holiday falling on a day that person does not work. It is worth nothing to them, and nothing was taken from them. See "Public holidays".

Hours count wherever the person recorded them. If the company keeps more than one area in Plane, hours from all of them are added together here, which is why a figure here can be larger than anything a single project shows.

### Balance

**Worked** minus **To work**, added up over everybody.

It is grey when it is exactly zero, green when the team is ahead, and red — with a red tint behind the card — when the team is behind. A person whose terms do not expect set hours each day contributes 0:00, whatever they worked.

### Still to close

Reads, for example, **3 of 4 people**.

The first number is how many people's months are not yet **Closed**. Every other state counts as still to close: **Not handed in**, **Handed in**, **Agreed** and **Reopened**. The second number is how many people are on the screen at all. When it reads 0 of 4, the month is finished.

## The table, column by column

The table is one row per person. It scrolls sideways inside its own box on a narrow screen.

| Column              | What it says                                                    |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Person**          | The person's name, with up to two marks beside it               |
| **State**           | How far through the month has got — one of five words           |
| **To work**         | What their terms ask of them, as described above                |
| **Worked**          | What their month came to, as described above                    |
| **Balance**         | Their **Worked** minus their **To work**, in green, red or grey |
| **Running balance** | Where their running balance stands after this month             |
| **Action**          | The one thing there is to do on this row, if anything           |

Two of these need saying properly.

**Running balance** is empty — a dash — for every month that has not been closed. Nothing on the screen explains that, and it is the single most common reason somebody thinks the screen is broken. The figure is worked out once, at the moment the month is closed, and never before:

    the running balance the month before closed with

- this month's Balance
  − hours taken off in lieu this month
  = the running balance after this month

"Hours taken off in lieu" means absences recorded under the reason **Time off in lieu**. Those hours are credited to Worked, which makes that day come out level, and then taken off the running balance — otherwise the person would be paid the hours and keep them as well.

Where the month before was never closed, the chain starts from the **Starting balance** recorded for that person on the People screen, and from nothing at all where there is none. That is why months have to be closed oldest first; see [When the screen refuses](#when-the-screen-refuses).

**The rows are in the order people were added, newest first.** They are not alphabetical, and there is no search box. Adding somebody pushes everybody down a row, so the row under your cursor this month may not be the row that was there last month. On a screen where one click agrees somebody's pay figures, read the name before you press anything.

## The marks beside a name

Two small marks can appear to the right of a person's name.

**A timer mark.** Its label reads "A timer is still running, so this month cannot be handed in yet." Somebody has a timer going on a work item, somewhere in this month, in any part of Plane. It counts nothing towards their Worked figure because it has no length yet. Only that person can stop it, on the work item itself. Note what this does and does not block: they cannot hand the month in while it runs, but you can still agree and close a month with a timer running in it.

**An orange warning triangle.** Its label reads "Some days need looking at before this month can be closed." It is a button — press it and the dialog **Days to look at for {person}** opens. It is there in every state, including on a month that is already closed, where settling a day changes nothing. See **Look at those days** below.

## The five states a month can be in

| On screen         | What it means                                                                                                       | What accepts changes                 | What happens next                               |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Not handed in** | The person has not handed the month in. It is worked out again from scratch every time somebody opens it.           | Their hours, absences and attendance | They press **Hand in** on their own My time     |
| **Handed in**     | They pressed **Hand in**. The month stops being worked out again.                                                   | Nothing                              | You press **Agree**                             |
| **Agreed**        | You said the figures are right. The figures are not frozen yet, and are not with payroll.                           | Nothing                              | You press **Close**                             |
| **Closed**        | The figures are frozen. The hours, absences and attendance behind them refuse to be changed.                        | Nothing                              | Nothing, unless something turns out to be wrong |
| **Reopened**      | A closed month that was put back. It is worked out again, and has to be handed in, agreed and closed a second time. | Their hours, absences and attendance | They press **Hand in** again                    |

Two things follow from that table and are worth knowing before you are asked about them.

**A month stops accepting changes the moment the person hands it in, not when it is closed.** From **Handed in** onwards, anybody trying to record hours, an absence or a day of attendance inside that month is told: "{month} has been handed in, so its records can no longer change. Ask whoever looks after the team to reopen it on Team time." That request comes to you. A month can only be reopened from **Closed**, so if it is only handed in and something has to change, the way through is to agree it, close it, and then reopen it.

**A reopened month never goes back to Not handed in.** It has its own state and stays in it until it is handed in, agreed and closed again. Both closings stay on the record.

## What each action does, and when it appears

The **Action** cell holds exactly one thing, chosen in this order. The first line that fits is what you get.

| The row's state                               | Flagged days? | What appears in the Action cell                        |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Handed in**                                 | either        | Blue **Agree** button                                  |
| **Closed**                                    | either        | **Reopen** link                                        |
| **Not handed in**, **Reopened** or **Agreed** | yes           | **Look at those days** button, with a warning triangle |
| **Agreed**                                    | no            | Blue **Close** button                                  |
| **Not handed in** or **Reopened**             | no            | Grey text, nothing to press                            |

### Agree

Shown on a row whose state is **Handed in**.

**Agreeing cannot be undone. There is no step back from Agreed to Handed in.** It happens on one click, with no question asked, on a row that looks like every other row. If you agree the wrong person's month, the only way out is to close that month and then reopen it with a written reason — and closing may itself be refused, for the reasons below. Read the name in the row before you press it.

When it works, a green **Agreed** message appears at the corner of the screen, the state changes to **Agreed**, and the button becomes **Close**.

**You cannot agree your own month.** Your own row carries the same blue **Agree** button as everybody else's, and pressing it always fails with the sentence "Approving your own month is not a decision. Ask someone else." Nothing on the screen warns you first. Somebody else who looks after the team has to do it. If there is nobody else, your own month cannot be agreed and therefore cannot be closed at all until a second person is given that role on the People screen.

### Close

Shown on a row whose state is **Agreed** and where no day is flagged.

Pressing it opens a dialog headed **Close {person}'s month?** which says:

> The figures are frozen as they are now. Nobody can change this month's hours or absences afterwards, and anything recorded into it later will not count. The month can be reopened, and the reopening is recorded.

Press **Close** in the dialog to go ahead, or **Cancel** to leave it. While it works the button reads **Closing…**.

Closing does four things:

1. It writes this month's totals onto the month, so they stop being worked out from the records underneath and are simply read from then on.
2. It keeps a record of what those figures were worked out from — the working week, the terms, the holiday calendar, the absences — so that in a year's time "why is March 148 hours" can be answered.
3. It stops the month being worked out again. This is what "frozen" means.
4. It marks the absences, the hours with no work item, and the attendance days behind it, so those records refuse to be edited.

Two consequences worth saying plainly. **Anything recorded into a closed month afterwards never counts towards it** — it is not refused and nothing warns anybody, it simply never reaches the figures, because the month is never worked out again. And **the Running balance column fills in for the first time**, which is the moment that person's running balance is fixed.

If the dialog stays on screen after you press **Close**, the month was refused; the red message says why, and the person's name is still in front of you.

### Reopen

Shown on a row whose state is **Closed**. It is a quiet link rather than a button, because it is not the ordinary thing to do.

Pressing it opens a dialog headed **Reopen {month}?** — for example, "Reopen October 2026?" — which says that the month has been closed and its figures frozen, and that reopening keeps the closed version and records this one alongside it. Underneath is a box labelled **Why is it being reopened?** The **Reopen** button stays greyed out until you have typed something. **Leave it closed** backs out.

What you type is kept with the month for good. Write the reason somebody would need in a year, not "fix".

Reopening unfreezes the month's hours, absences and attendance, sets the state to **Reopened**, and puts the row back to waiting for that person. They correct what was wrong, hand the month in again, and you agree and close it a second time.

**Before you reopen an older month, check whether any of that person's later months are already closed.** Each month's running balance is worked out once, when it is closed, from the running balance the month before it closed with. Reopen October and close it again on a different figure, and a November that was already closed keeps the running balance it was closed with. Nothing corrects it and nothing warns you, and that wrong figure has already gone to the accountant. If later months are closed, reopen those too — then close them again in order, oldest first.

### Look at those days

Shown on a row that has at least one flagged day, unless the state is **Handed in** or **Closed**. It is also reachable at any time from the orange triangle beside the person's name.

A day gets flagged when hours the month had already counted are no longer there. The commonest cause is a work item being deleted, which takes every timer on it — including other people's — with it. The system cannot tell whether the hours were withdrawn on purpose or lost by accident, so it will not guess, and the month will not close until somebody says.

Pressing it opens **Days to look at for {person}**, which says:

> Hours that were counted on these days are no longer there. Writing down what happened lets the month be closed, but it does not put the hours back — if they should still count, have them recorded again first. What you write is kept with the day, and the person it is about can read it.

There is one card per flagged day. Each card shows the date, a figure reading **Counts as 6:00 now** — what that day comes to after the loss — a box labelled **What happened?**, and a **Settle this day** button that stays greyed out until you type something. The days are settled one at a time, each with its own answer.

Settling **changes no figure at all**. It records that the question was asked and answered. Once the last day is settled, the card list empties, the message **Nothing left to look at. This month can be closed.** takes its place, the warning triangle beside the name goes away, and the ordinary action comes back.

The button at the bottom of the dialog reads **Done** and closes the dialog. It does not close the month.

If the hours ought to still count, have them recorded again before you settle the day. On a month that is still open, the **Hours in detail** screen shows only the hours that are there today, so it cannot tell you what was removed; see "Hours in detail".

### When there is nothing to press

A row whose state is **Not handed in** or **Reopened**, with no flagged days, shows grey text instead of a button:

- **The month is not over yet** — while the month on screen has not ended.
- **Waiting for {person} to hand the month in** — once it has.

**There is no way to hand a month in from this screen.** Handing in is that person's own act, on their own **My time**, and it cannot be done for them here. Nothing tells them it is waiting either — no email, no notice in the product. If a row sits on "Waiting for…", ask the person.

## The month from start to finish

The routine, once a month has ended:

1. Open **Absences** and deal with every row still showing **Asked for**. A request that has not been agreed changes nothing at all — not the month, not the balance, not the leave. See "Time away from work".
2. Open **Team time**. It opens on last month, which is usually the one you want. Check the month name.
3. Look down the **State** column. Rows reading **Not handed in** are waiting on the person. Ask them to hand the month in.
4. On each row reading **Handed in**, check the figures, then press **Agree**. Check the name first — this cannot be undone.
5. Deal with any orange triangle: press it, say what happened on each day, press **Settle this day** for each, then **Done**.
6. On each row now reading **Agreed**, press **Close** and confirm. The **Running balance** column fills in.
7. When **Still to close** reads 0 of however many people there are, the month is finished.
8. Press the button with three dots and choose **Download as Excel** or **Download as CSV**, and send the file to the payroll accountant.

Months have to be closed oldest first. If a row refuses with a message naming an earlier month, go back to that month and close it, then return.

## A worked month, with the sums

October 2026. Anna is on site, full time, 7:42 a day, Monday to Friday. October has 21 working days for her. There is one public holiday, on a Monday she would have worked, and she took two days of annual leave.

**What the month asks of her — To work.**

    21 working days × 7:42  =  21 × 462 minutes  =  9,702 minutes  =  161:42

The public holiday does not come off that, and neither do the two days of leave. Her To work stays 161:42.

**What her month came to — Worked.**

| Where it came from                             | Hours and minutes |   Minutes |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------: | --------: |
| Timers on work items                           |            128:00 |     7,680 |
| Hours with no work item — meetings, admin      |              8:00 |       480 |
| The public holiday, credited as her day        |              7:42 |       462 |
| Two days of annual leave, credited as her days |             15:24 |       924 |
| **Worked**                                     |        **159:06** | **9,546** |

**Her Balance.**

    9,546 − 9,702  =  −156 minutes  =  −2:36

Shown in red. She is two hours thirty-six minutes behind for October.

**Her Running balance.** September closed at +3:00, that is 180 minutes. She took no hours off in lieu in October. So once October is closed:

    180 + (−156) − 0  =  24 minutes  =  +0:24

Until October is closed, that cell reads a dash.

**On the screen, on 12 November 2026,** with four people employed — Anna, Daniel, Sofia, and Jonas who invoices his own hours:

| Person | State     | To work | Worked | Balance | Running balance | Action     |
| ------ | --------- | ------: | -----: | ------: | --------------: | ---------- |
| Anna   | Handed in |  161:42 | 159:06 |   -2:36 |               — | **Agree**  |
| Daniel | Agreed    |  161:42 | 165:12 |   +3:30 |               — | **Close**  |
| Sofia  | Closed    |  161:42 | 161:42 |    0:00 |          +12:00 | **Reopen** |
| Jonas  | Handed in |    0:00 |  88:15 |    0:00 |               — | **Agree**  |

The four cards above it read:

- **To work** 485:06 — Anna, Daniel and Sofia at 161:42 each; Jonas adds nothing.
- **Worked** 574:15 — 9,546 + 9,912 + 9,702 + 5,295 = 34,455 minutes.
- **Balance** +0:54 — −156 + 210 + 0 + 0 = 54 minutes.
- **Still to close** 3 of 4 people — everybody except Sofia.

Sofia's row is worth reading twice. Her Worked exactly equals her To work and her Balance is 0:00 — she was away for most of the month on agreed leave, and the leave was credited to Worked. Jonas's To work and Balance are 0:00 because nothing is owed of him by the day; only his Worked figure means anything.

The small print under the table reads:

> Everyone's hours are counted wherever they logged them, as hours and minutes: 161:12 means 161 hours and 12 minutes. The running balance only gets a figure once a month is closed. Closing a month freezes its figures; nothing recorded afterwards changes them.

Because October has ended, that is all of it. While a month is still running, one more sentence appears in front of it, naming the day the figures reach: "Figures are as things stand on Wed, 12 Nov; the month is not over."

## When the screen refuses

When **Agree**, **Close** or **Reopen** does not go through, a red message appears at the corner of the screen headed **That did not go through**, with a sentence underneath. **That sentence always comes back in English, even when the rest of the screen is in German.** These are the ones you will meet.

| The sentence                                                                                                                                                                   | What has happened                                                                                   | What to do                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Only a submitted month can be approved."                                                                                                                                      | The row is out of date. Somebody else has already agreed it, or the person withdrew it.             | The table refreshes itself after every action; read the row again                              |
| "Approving your own month is not a decision. Ask someone else."                                                                                                                | You pressed **Agree** on your own row.                                                              | Somebody else who looks after the team has to agree it                                         |
| "Only an approved month can be closed."                                                                                                                                        | The row is out of date, or the month has not been agreed.                                           | Read the row again; agree it first                                                             |
| "This month is still running. It can be closed from 2026-11-01."                                                                                                               | The month has not ended yet where that person is. The date given is the first day it can be closed. | Wait until the month has ended                                                                 |
| "September 2026 has not been closed yet, and this month opens on what that one closes with. Close it first, or the balance carried into this month starts again from nothing." | The month before this one is not closed, so the running balance has nothing to carry forward from.  | Step back to the month it names, finish it, then come back. It may be more than one month back |
| "Some days are marked for review because hours counted earlier are no longer there. Settle those before closing the month."                                                    | A day is flagged.                                                                                   | Press the orange triangle beside the name, settle every day, then close                        |
| "Only a closed month can be reopened."                                                                                                                                         | The row is out of date.                                                                             | Reload the screen                                                                              |
| "No such month."                                                                                                                                                               | Two people are working at once, or the page has been open a long time.                              | Reload the screen                                                                              |
| "You don't have the required permissions."                                                                                                                                     | Your account is not set up as looking after the team.                                               | Ask whoever set your account up                                                                |

Empty months do not block closing. A month from before somebody was employed, or one that only exists because you stepped through it, holds nothing and is skipped. A month that has ever been closed always blocks, however empty it looks.

## The two files you can download

At the far right of that row is a button with three dots. It holds two items:

- **Download as CSV** — a plain file most things can open
- **Download as Excel** — a spreadsheet file

Both give the same figures for the month on screen. The file is named after the month: `hr-2026-10.xlsx` or `hr-2026-10.csv`.

**Before you press either: the file replaces the whole page in the same tab.** When it downloads normally you will not notice. When it does not — the session has expired, or something failed — the application disappears and you are left looking at a page of raw text. Use the browser's Back button to get the application back. Nothing is lost.

The file holds one line per person, in order of email address. **That is not the order of the rows on screen**, which are in the order people were added, newest first. Do not check the screen against the file line by line; match on the name.

**The file includes people who have left; the screen does not.** More on that below.

### What the file says the state is

The State column in the file uses different words from the screen. This matters, because the accountant reads the file and never sees the screen.

| In the file | On the screen | Means                                                 |
| ----------- | ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Open        | Not handed in | The person has not handed it in                       |
| Submitted   | Handed in     | Handed in, not yet agreed                             |
| Approved    | Agreed        | Agreed, not yet closed                                |
| Locked      | Closed        | **Frozen. Only this one means the figures are final** |
| Reopened    | Reopened      | It was closed and has been put back                   |

### The columns

Every column ending in `_minutes` is a whole number of minutes. Every column ending in `_hours` is the same quantity as decimal hours to two places — 9,702 minutes appears as `161.7`. The decimal columns are there for reading and copying; the minute columns are the exact ones.

| Column                                             | What it holds                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `employee`, `email`                                | Who the line is about                                                                         |
| `period_start`, `period_end`                       | The first and last day of the month                                                           |
| `state`                                            | See the table above                                                                           |
| `target_minutes`, `target_hours`                   | The month's **To work**                                                                       |
| `actual_minutes`, `actual_hours`                   | The month's **Worked**                                                                        |
| `balance_minutes`, `balance_hours`                 | **Worked** minus **To work**                                                                  |
| `project_minutes`                                  | The part of Worked that came from timers on work items                                        |
| `non_project_minutes`                              | The part that came from hours with no work item                                               |
| `absence_minutes`                                  | The part that came from paid time off                                                         |
| `holiday_minutes`                                  | The part that came from public holidays                                                       |
| `leave_consumed_minutes`                           | How much annual leave the month spent                                                         |
| `opening_balance_minutes`                          | The running balance the month began on                                                        |
| `closing_balance_minutes`                          | The running balance after this month — empty until it is closed                               |
| `counted_through`                                  | How far into the month the figures reach. Empty for a month that has ended                    |
| `balance_minutes_to_date`, `balance_hours_to_date` | Where the person stood at that date. The same as the balance above for a month that has ended |
| `telework_days`                                    | Days worked from home in this month                                                           |
| `telework_days_this_year`                          | Days worked from home in the calendar year up to the end of this month                        |

The first four `_minutes` components — project, non-project, absence, holiday — add up to `actual_minutes`. That is the arithmetic to show anybody who asks why the hours figure is higher than the hours somebody was at a desk.

## Things that are not what they look like

- **It opens on last month.** My time opens on this one. Read the month name.
- **There is no sidebar entry.** The way here is the three-dot menu on My time.
- **Your own row has an Agree button that always fails.** Somebody else has to agree your month. You can close and reopen your own month; only agreeing is blocked.
- **Somebody who has been marked as having left disappears from this screen, for every month, including the months they worked.** Their final month — the one payroll is waiting for — can then not be agreed or closed from here at all. The file sent to the accountant is not filtered the same way and still contains them, with whatever state their month had reached. **Finish somebody's last month before marking them as left on the People screen.**
- **Worked includes paid time off and public holidays.** To work is never reduced.
- **To work is only the month so far while the month is running.** The whole-month figure is in the small label on the table cell.
- **Self-invoicing people show To work 0:00 and Balance 0:00 for ever.** Only their Worked figure means anything.
- **Running balance is a dash until the month is closed.**
- **The rows are newest-added first; the file is in email order.**
- **Refusals come back in English** whatever language the screen is in.
- **The three-dot menu and the links are shown even to somebody who is refused the figures.** Pressing an export there replaces the page with a refusal in raw text.
- **A running timer counts nothing** towards Worked, and does not stop you agreeing or closing — only the person's own handing in.
- **A flagged day blocks closing but not agreeing.** A month can be handed in and agreed with days still flagged.
- **Stepping through months writes empty months for everybody.** It is harmless; empty months do not block anything from closing.
- **Nothing chases anybody.** No email or notice is sent when a month is waiting to be handed in, agreed or closed. That is your job.

- Who is employed, on what terms, whether they expect set hours each day, and who looks after the team: **"People and their terms"**.
- Agreeing time off, and why an absence still showing **Asked for** counts for nothing: **"Time away from work"** and **"Leave"**.
- Days off for everybody, and why a change to them does not reach a closed month: **"Public holidays"**.
- Filling in months from before the system was in use: **"Bringing hours in from somewhere else"**.
- Something on screen that this page does not explain: **"When something goes wrong"**.
