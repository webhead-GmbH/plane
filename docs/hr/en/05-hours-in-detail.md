# Hours in detail

This page is for anybody who has looked at a monthly figure and wanted to know what is behind it. **Hours in detail** is the one screen that lists a month's hours entry by entry. It answers questions like "the month says 132 hours, on what?", "why does this column not add up?", "where did the hour I logged on Tuesday go?" and "what does that struck-through line mean?".

The screen only shows. It changes nothing, and nothing on it can be edited, deleted or sent anywhere.

## What this screen is for

Your month is normally one number a day. That says how much, and nothing else.

**Hours in detail** takes the same month and lists every hour that went into it, one line per entry, and lets you gather those lines five different ways: by day, by work item, by project, by week or by workspace. Underneath the main table it lists the hours that belong to no work item at all — meetings, admin, training, travel, corrections.

It reads the records. It does not rebuild anything and it does not correct anything. Opening it can never change a figure somebody else has already read.

## How to open it

There is no entry for it in the sidebar. There are two ways in.

**If you look after your own hours only:**

1. Open **My time** from the sidebar.
2. At the top right there are the **Record time for today** button, sometimes a **Hand in** button, and then a small button with three dots on it. The three-dot button has no words on it.
3. Press the three-dot button. A short menu opens.
4. Choose **Hours in detail**. It is near the bottom of the menu.

You land on the current month, your own hours, grouped by **Day**.

**If you look after the team:**

1. Open **Team time** from the sidebar.
2. At the top of the screen, to the right of the month arrows, there is a row of five links. **Hours in detail** is the first of them. Press it.

You land on the current month, **your own** hours, grouped by **Day** — not on whoever you were looking at on **Team time**, and not on the month you were looking at either. Nothing is carried across. **Team time** opens on last month, so this screen usually starts a month later than the one you just left; step back with the left arrow. Change the person with the first of the two boxes at the top right, described below.

At the very top of the page there is a trail reading **Team time** then **Hours in detail**. The words **Team time** are a link, and they are shown to everybody. If you do not look after the team, do not press them: **Team time** is only readable by whoever does, and following the link gets you a screen saying **This is not yours to see**. Use your browser's back button to return, or open **My time** from the sidebar.

## The controls at the top

Across the top there is a month name with an arrow either side of it, and one or two plain boxes on the right.

### The month

The month name sits between the two arrows and is not a button — you cannot type in it.

- The left arrow steps back one month.
- The right arrow steps forward one month.

Neither arrow ever stops working. You can step back to a month before the person was even employed, and forward into months that have not happened yet. Both look exactly like a month in which nothing was recorded. So before you report that somebody logged nothing in January, check on **People and their terms** when they started.

After you press an arrow, the month name changes and the table refreshes. The old table stays on screen for a moment until the new one arrives. That is normal.

### The boxes on the right

There is no writing next to these boxes. You have to know what they are.

| Who sees it                            | Which box                                                               | What it does                       |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Everybody                              | The **Group by** box, on the far right, reading **Day** when you arrive | Chooses how the lines are gathered |
| Only somebody who looks after the team | A second box to the left of it, reading **My hours** when you arrive    | Chooses whose hours are shown      |

If you see only one box, you are looking at your own hours and there is no way to look at anybody else's. That is correct, not a fault.

If you look after the team, the first box lists **My hours** and then every person with an employment record. Three things about that list are worth knowing:

- It is in the order the records were created, newest first. It is **not** alphabetical, and there is no search. With eight or ten people, read the whole list.
- People who have left are still in it. Their closed months still have to be readable.
- If the list shows **My hours** and nothing else, and you know there are other people, the list failed to load. Reload the page.

Picking a different person reloads the whole screen for that person. The month you were on and the grouping you chose are kept.

## The five ways of grouping

The **Group by** box has five options, always in this order.

| Option        | The lines are gathered by                     | Order of the groups            | Open or closed to start with |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| **Day**       | Each calendar day                             | Newest day first               | Open                         |
| **Work item** | Each work item the hours were logged against  | Most hours first, then by name | Closed                       |
| **Project**   | Each project                                  | Most hours first, then by name | Closed                       |
| **Week**      | Each week, named after the Monday it began on | Newest week first              | Closed                       |
| **Workspace** | Each workspace                                | Most hours first, then by name | Closed                       |

**Day** is what you arrive on and is the shape most people read a month in. Every day is already open. The other four arrive collapsed, so you get a short list to scan first; press a group line to open it.

A week is named **Week of** and then a date — for example **Week of 2 Mar 2026**. That date is always the Monday the week started, whether or not anybody worked on it.

Which one you want depends on the question:

- "What did I do on Tuesday?" — **Day**
- "How long did that one job take?" — **Work item**
- "Which customer took the month?" — **Project**
- "Was last week heavier than the one before?" — **Week**
- "How much of this came from the other workspace?" — **Workspace**

Opening and closing groups is remembered while you stay on the screen, separately for each person, each month and each grouping. It is forgotten when you reload the page or go somewhere else.

## The four figures

Four cards sit above the table.

### 1. **On work items**

The total of every hour logged against a work item in this month, as hours and minutes. `132:06` means 132 hours and 6 minutes.

- It counts hours logged in **every** workspace and every project, including projects you are not a member of.
- It does **not** count hours with no work item — those are the next card.
- It does **not** count paid time off or public holidays.
- A timer that is still running counts nothing at all here. It has no length yet.

It is worked out by exactly the same rule the month itself uses, so it can be compared with **My time** without allowing for any difference. There is one exception, described under [An entry has been deleted](#an-entry-has-been-deleted) below.

### 2. **Not on a work item**

The total of the hours in the second table further down — meetings, administration, training, travel, on call, corrections and anything brought in from somewhere else.

These are held as whole minutes, and they carry a sign. A **Correction** of minus 30 minutes makes this figure 30 minutes smaller. That is the one way a recorded figure comes back down.

### 3. **Entries on work items**

A plain count, not a length. It is the number of lines the main table holds.

It is easy to misread, so precisely:

- It counts every line in the main table, including a timer still running (which counts no minutes) and including a struck-through line for something that has since been deleted.
- It does **not** count anything in **Hours with no work item**.
- It does **not** count entries the month recorded that can no longer be found — see the second note below.

So it is not "the number of things on this page". It is the number of work-item lines.

### 4. **This month**

This card does not show a length. It shows the month's own state as one of five words, with a short line underneath saying whether the hours can still change.

| The card says     | What it means                                                                              | The line underneath                  |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| **Not handed in** | Nobody has handed the month in yet. This is also what a month nobody has ever opened says. | **These hours can still change**     |
| **Handed in**     | The person has handed the month in.                                                        | **These hours can no longer change** |
| **Agreed**        | Whoever looks after the team has agreed it.                                                | **These hours can no longer change** |
| **Closed**        | The month is finished and its figures have gone to payroll.                                | **These hours can no longer change** |
| **Reopened**      | A month that had been closed and was put back so something could be corrected.             | **These hours can still change**     |

The state also decides where the lines come from, and this matters more than it looks:

- **Not handed in** and **Reopened**: the table shows the hours as they stand **today**.
- **Handed in**, **Agreed** and **Closed**: the table shows what the month **counted** when it stopped being rebuilt. Nothing recorded afterwards appears in it.

That is why an hour you add today never turns up in a month that has been handed in, and why a month that has been handed in can show lines for entries that were later deleted.

What each state means for the month as a whole, and how a month moves between them, is on "The month, from open to closed".

## The two notes under the figures

Two short lines can appear under the four cards. They are the two things the figures cannot say on their own.

### A timer is still running

> 1 timer is still running, so it counts nothing yet. Stop it on the work item. The month cannot be handed in while it runs.

The line appears whenever at least one entry in this month has no length yet, and the wording changes for more than one.

The timer is shown in the table with a small clock beside the name, and **How long** for that line reads `—`. Stop it on the work item it belongs to, then come back. "Recording hours" says how.

### An entry has been deleted

> 1 entry this month counted has since been deleted. Its hours are not in the figures here, but the month still counts them.

This one only ever appears on a month that has been handed in, agreed or closed. Read the sentence carefully, because it is not the same as the struck-through lines described below:

- Here the entry is gone completely. Only a reference to it was kept, so there is no name, no note and no length to put on screen. Nothing can be recovered.
- The month's own total still includes those hours, because the month counted them before they disappeared. So **On work items** on this screen is a little smaller than the figure the month is closing with.

Nothing you can do on this screen fixes it. If a month is refusing to close because hours that were counted are no longer there, that is dealt with on **Team time**, which lists the days concerned and asks for a written answer on each. See "The month, from open to closed".

## The table of hours on work items

Four columns, in this order: **What**, **When**, **Note**, **How long**.

On a phone the table is wider than the screen, and **How long** is off the right-hand edge. Drag the table sideways to see it. There is no way to reach it with the keyboard, so for anything more than a glance use a larger screen.

### The group line

Every group has a line of its own. It reads:

- a small arrow, pointing down when the group is open and right when it is closed,
- the name of the group,
- then the number of lines in it, written as **· 12 entries** (or **· 1 entry**),
- and on the far right, the group's total.

Press anywhere on that line to open or close the group.

A day group is named like **Mon 2 Mar**. Note it carries no year — only the month name at the top of the screen tells you which March. A week group carries its year: **Week of 2 Mar 2026**.

If a work item, project or workspace has no name on it, the group is called **Not named**.

### An entry line

| Column       | What is in it                                                                                                                            |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What**     | The work item's short key, such as `WEB-142`, then its name, then any marks (below). Underneath, in smaller grey text, the project name. |
| **When**     | The day — but only when you are **not** grouped by **Day**, because the group line already says the day. Then any marks.                 |
| **Note**     | Whatever was typed against the entry, unchanged. Often empty.                                                                            |
| **How long** | That one entry's own length.                                                                                                             |

Within a group the newest entry is at the top.

### The marks on a line

| What you see                                                                              | What it means                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **· added by hand** in the **When** column                                                | The entry was typed in afterwards rather than timed with a timer.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **· stopped by the system, needs correcting** in the **When** column, in a warning colour | The timer was left running and the system stopped it at the longest time it allows. Hover it and it says: "This timer was left running, so the system stopped it at the longest time it allows. That is a limit, not time that was worked. Correct it on the work item before the month is handed in." The figure beside it is a ceiling, not a measurement. |
| A small clock beside the name, and `—` under **How long**                                 | The timer is still running. It has no length yet and counts nothing.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| The whole line greyed out and struck through, with a warning triangle                     | The entry was deleted after the month counted it. Hover the triangle and it says: "This entry was deleted after the month counted it. Its hours are still in the figures above." The total is right; the line is shown so you can see why.                                                                                                                   |
| **Not named** where a name should be                                                      | The work item has no name on it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **under a minute** under **How long**                                                     | The entry is shorter than half a minute. It is written in words rather than as `0:00`, so it does not read as a mistake. Those seconds still count towards the day.                                                                                                                                                                                          |

A struck-through line can only appear on a month that has been handed in, agreed or closed. **While a month is still open, hours that have been deleted are not on this screen at all** — no struck-through line, no count, nothing. There is no screen that shows what was removed from an open month. If you are trying to work out why an open month is short, this screen cannot tell you.

## Hours with no work item

Below the main table, when there are any, comes a second section headed **Hours with no work item**, with this line under it:

> Meetings, administration, training, travel, corrections — and hours brought in from somewhere else. They count in the month too, but they are not in the table above. A correction with a minus sign takes hours off the month.

If there are none this month, the whole section is missing. There is no empty message for it. That is normal for most people.

The columns here are **When**, **What**, **Note**, **How long** — note that **When** and **What** are the other way round from the main table.

- **When** is the day the hours were recorded against.
- **What** is one of **Internal meeting**, **Training**, **Administration**, **Travel**, **On call**, **Correction** or **Imported**.
- **Note** is whatever was typed.
- **How long** carries a sign. A correction of 45 minutes reads `-0:45`.

There is no grouping, no opening and closing, and no subtotal line here. The rows are newest first. Their total is the **Not on a work item** card at the top.

How these are entered is on "Recording hours". Where the imported ones came from is on "Bringing hours in from somewhere else".

## Why the subtotals need not add up

This is the thing people distrust most, so here is exactly what happens.

Hours from timers are recorded in **seconds**. The month does not round each entry. It adds up all the seconds for **one person, on one day, on one project**, and rounds that one total to the nearest minute, half a minute upwards. That is deliberate: rounding every entry on its own throws away up to half a minute each time, and somebody who logs thirty short entries a month would drift visibly away from what they actually worked.

Two consequences follow, and both look like errors until you know the rule.

**The lines inside a group need not add up to the group's total.** Each line shows its own length, rounded on its own for display. The total was rounded once, over the whole day and project.

**The group totals need not add up to the figure at the top — but only when grouping by Work item.** A week is a whole number of days, and a project sits in exactly one workspace, so **Day**, **Week**, **Project** and **Workspace** can never split one of those day-and-project totals. **Work item** can, because one project's day often holds entries against several work items.

When you group by **Work item**, and only then, an extra sentence appears at the bottom of the screen in front of the usual one:

> Each day's hours on a project are rounded once, so adding these lines up can come out a minute or two from the figure above. The figure above is the one that counts.

Here is a case. On one Tuesday, Anna logged three entries against three work items in the same project:

| Work item | Actually recorded            | Shown as |
| --------- | ---------------------------- | -------- |
| WEB-142   | 1 hour 12 minutes 29 seconds | `1:12`   |
| WEB-155   | 48 minutes 29 seconds        | `0:48`   |
| WEB-160   | 25 minutes 29 seconds        | `0:25`   |

Grouped by **Work item**, those three lines come to 1:12 + 0:48 + 0:25 = **2:25**.

Grouped by **Day**, the same three entries are added as seconds first: 2 hours 26 minutes and 27 seconds, which rounds to **2:26**.

One minute apart, and 2:26 is the figure the month counts. Each entry was 29 seconds past a whole minute, so on its own each rounded down; together they were nearly a minute and a half, which rounded up.

### The last line on the screen

At the very bottom, always:

> Hours are counted wherever they were logged, in every workspace. Paid leave and public holidays also count towards the month, and are not shown here.

Both halves matter.

**"in every workspace"** — a workspace is one of the separate areas the company keeps its projects in, and there is more than one of them. Hours from all of them are counted together into one month. That is why a figure here can be larger than anything a single project shows, and why somebody looking after the team can see work-item names, project names and notes from projects they are not a member of. Nothing is being counted anywhere you cannot see; it is being counted from more places than you may have expected.

**"Paid leave and public holidays"** — a day of agreed leave and a public holiday both count towards your month, but neither is an entry, so neither is on this screen. That is why **On work items** plus **Not on a work item** is smaller than **Worked** on **My time** in any month containing time off. The worked example below shows the whole sum.

## Which day an hour counts on

An hour counts on the day its timer was **started**. An entry typed in by hand counts on the day it was written down.

That has one consequence worth remembering: a timer started at 23:40 on 31 March and stopped at 00:20 on 1 April counts **wholly in March**, including all the minutes after midnight. If March has already been handed in, those minutes are inside a month that can no longer change. Looking for them on 1 April finds nothing.

The day is worked out by the clock where the person whose hours they are is, not by the clock where the reader is. Somebody reading a colleague's month from another country sees the same days that colleague sees.

Hours with no work item are different: they carry a plain date that somebody typed, so no question of timezones arises for them at all.

## What this screen cannot do

Say this once and it saves an afternoon: **nothing on this screen can be changed from this screen.** There is no edit, no delete, no export and no print. The work item key, such as `WEB-142`, is plain text and not a link — you cannot jump from a line to the work item it belongs to.

To correct an hour that is on a work item, go to that work item and correct it in the list of logged time there. Its start, its end and its note can all be changed in place. "Recording hours" explains how.

An hour in **Hours with no work item** works differently. It cannot be changed at all. The only way to correct one is to remove it and enter it again.

**Removing an entry cannot be undone.** There is no restore and no bin to fetch it back from. Before you remove anything, write down the day, the length, the category and the note, so you can type it back correctly.

**If the month has been handed in, agreed or closed, nothing in it can be changed at all.** Look at the **This month** card. If it says **These hours can no longer change**, an attempt to correct anything in that month will be refused with a message telling you to ask whoever looks after the team to reopen it. Reopening is done on **Team time** and is described in "The month, from open to closed". Do not try to make up the difference by entering the hours in a later month — that puts them in the wrong month for payroll.

Two more things this screen does not carry:

- The month, the person and the grouping are **not** in the address bar. Sending somebody the link does not send them what you are looking at — they get the current month, their own hours, grouped by **Day**. Tell them in words which month and which grouping to choose.
- There is no search, no filter and no sorting by column.

## A whole month worked through

Anna is due 7 hours 42 minutes a day, Monday to Friday. March has 21 working days for her. One public holiday falls on a Thursday she would have worked, and she took two days of leave.

**What her month owes.** 7:42 is 462 minutes. 462 × 21 = 9,702 minutes, which is 161:42. That is the **To work** figure on **My time**.

**What this screen shows her.** She opens **My time**, presses the three-dot button, chooses **Hours in detail**, and steps back one month with the left arrow. She sees:

- **On work items** `132:06`
- **Not on a work item** `6:30`
- **Entries on work items** `96`
- **This month** **Handed in**, and underneath, **These hours can no longer change**

**Putting it together with My time.**

|                                          | Hours and minutes | In minutes |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ---------- |
| **On work items**, from this screen      | 132:06            | 7,926      |
| **Not on a work item**, from this screen | 6:30              | 390        |
| One whole public holiday                 | 7:42              | 462        |
| Two days of leave                        | 15:24             | 924        |
| **Worked**, on My time                   | **161:42**        | **9,702**  |
| **To work**, on My time                  | **161:42**        | **9,702**  |
| **Balance**                              | **0:00**          | **0**      |

The two figures on this screen come to 138:36 between them. The remaining 23:06 is the holiday and the leave, which are counted in her month but do not appear here, exactly as the last line on the screen says. A public holiday is worth what that person would have worked that day, so it is a different number for everybody — "Public holidays" explains that, including who it is worth nothing to.

**Which customer took the month.** She changes **Group by** to **Project**. The groups arrive closed and biggest first:

```
▸ Website     · 58 entries                                74:12
▸ CRM         · 31 entries                                48:20
▸ Internal    ·  7 entries                                 9:34
```

74:12 is 4,452 minutes, 48:20 is 2,900, 9:34 is 574. Together 7,926 — exactly **On work items**. Grouping by project never splits a day-and-project total, so this adds up to the minute, and the bottom of the screen shows only the usual sentence.

**Which job took the month.** She changes **Group by** to **Work item**. The extra sentence about rounding appears at the bottom, and the work-item totals come to a minute or two away from 132:06. That is the rounding described above, and 132:06 is still the figure that counts.

**The other table.** Scrolling down, **Hours with no work item** reads:

```
When         What                Note                        How long
Fri 27 Mar   Internal meeting    Quarterly planning              2:00
Wed 18 Mar   Training            Security refresher              3:00
Mon  9 Mar   Travel              Site visit, Linz                2:00
Tue  3 Mar   Correction          Double-booked on 2 Mar         -0:30
```

2:00 + 3:00 + 2:00 − 0:30 = 6:30, which is the **Not on a work item** card. The minus sign on the correction is doing real work: it takes half an hour off the month.

**The line she was actually looking for.** On Thursday 12 March there is a line greyed out and struck through, with a warning triangle. Hovering it says the entry was deleted after the month counted it, and that its hours are still in the figures above. The 3:20 it contributed is inside the 132:06, because March has been handed in and was counted on what was there at the time. Her question is answered without asking anybody.

## If you get stuck

- **You are new and none of this is familiar** — "Start here".
- **A figure on this screen does not match your month** — read "My time — your own month", which explains **To work**, **Worked** and **Balance**, and how leave and public holidays are counted into them.
- **You need to correct an hour, or add one** — "Recording hours".
- **You expected these hours to match the times you came in and went home** — they are two separate records and are not meant to agree. "When you were at work" explains why.
- **You want to know what happens at the end of the month, or how a month gets reopened** — "The month, from open to closed".
- **You look after the team and want everybody's month at once** — "Team time — everybody's month".
- **You are checking somebody's start date, their working week or their hours a day** — "People and their terms".
- **A day of leave or a sick day is missing** — "Time away from work" and "Leave".
- **A public holiday is not counted, or is counted for the wrong amount** — "Public holidays".
- **Hours were brought in from a spreadsheet and something looks wrong** — "Bringing hours in from somewhere else".
- **Something is refusing to work and none of the above fits** — "When something goes wrong".

If none of those settles it, ask whoever looks after the team. This screen is a read of the records, so anything wrong on it is wrong in the records, and that is where it has to be put right.
