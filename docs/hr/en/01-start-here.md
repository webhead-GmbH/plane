# Start here

This page is for anybody who has been given an account, been told to record their working time in it, and never been shown how. It says what the system is, who can see what, what the words on the screens mean, how a length of time is written, and what happens to a month between the day it begins and the day it is finished with. Every other page in this handbook assumes you have read this one.

## What this is

Your working time is recorded in Plane, in the same place as the work itself. There is no second program and no spreadsheet.

Every person has one record per month. That record holds one row for every day of the month, and three figures at the top: what you were meant to work, what you worked, and the difference between them.

Three things go into what you worked.

1. **Hours from timers on work items.** You start and stop those on the work item itself, inside a project. They arrive on your month by themselves. You never re-enter them.
2. **Hours you add by hand**, for time that no work item accounts for — meetings, administration, training, travel. On the screen these are called **Hours with no work item**.
3. **Time off and public holidays.** You do not enter these yourself. An absence is recorded by whoever looks after the team; a public holiday comes from the company calendar. Both count as hours worked. That is deliberate, and [Figures that look wrong and are not](#figures-that-look-wrong-and-are-not) explains why.

Separately from all of that, some people's records also keep the plain fact of **when you were at work** — the time you started, the time you stopped, and how long you broke for. That is a record the law asks to be kept. It is not part of the hours arithmetic and is never added to it.

A month goes through four steps: it is open, you hand it in, somebody else agrees it, and then it is closed. Once it is closed its figures are frozen, and those frozen figures are what goes to the payroll accountant.

## Where to find it

1. In the left sidebar of any workspace, press **My time**. It has a clock icon and it is always there.
2. Your own month opens, on the current month.

That is the only entry in the sidebar. Everything else in the system is reached from **My time**, through the **…** button at the top right of the screen.

Whoever looks after the team has one extra item in that menu — **Everyone’s hours** — which opens **Team time**, where everybody's month is side by side. Nobody else has that item, and nobody else can open that screen.

### One set of figures, whichever workspace you are in

Plane can hold more than one workspace: separate areas with their own projects, which you switch between at the top of the sidebar. Working time is **not** kept per workspace. You see the same month whichever workspace you happen to be in, and a timer you started in any of them counts towards it.

This is worth knowing because it explains a figure that otherwise looks too big: the hours on your month can be more than the hours any single project shows, because hours from every workspace are counted together.

## Who can see what

Two things decide what you see, and neither of them is your role in the workspace.

- **Whether you have an employment record.** Without one, there is nothing to show you. You will see **No employment record here** and, under it, _"Your hours are not being tracked yet. If they should be, ask whoever looks after the team to set you up."_ Ask them; nothing you can press will fix it.
- **Whether your record says you look after the team.** That is a setting on your own record. It is not the same as being a workspace administrator, and creating a workspace does not give it to you.

| Screen                                                                    | If you look after only your own hours                                                  | If you look after the team                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **My time** — your own month, day by day                                  | Yours                                                                                  | Yours. Other people's months are on **Team time**, not here |
| **Team time** — everybody's month                                         | Refused                                                                                | Everybody, in every workspace                               |
| **People** — who is employed, on what terms, and what their time is worth | Refused                                                                                | Everybody                                                   |
| **Absences** — time away from work                                        | Your own absences only, and there is no link to the screen from anywhere you can reach | Everybody's, and only you can agree or refuse a request     |
| **Public holidays** — the company calendar                                | You can read it                                                                        | You can read it and change it                               |
| **Hours in detail** — your hours entry by entry                           | Your own                                                                               | Anybody's; you choose whose                                 |

If you open **Team time** without being set up to look after the team, you get **This is not yours to see**, and under it _"Everyone's hours are only readable by whoever looks after the team. Your own month is on My time."_ That is not a fault and there is nothing to retry. It is the system telling you the screen is not yours.

Your colleagues cannot read your figures, your absences or the reasons for them. Only whoever looks after the team can, and they can read everybody's.

## The words used everywhere

These words mean the same thing on every screen. Where two screens use different words for one thing, it is said here.

| On screen                   | What it means                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **My time**                 | Your own month.                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Team time**               | Everybody's month, on one screen. Only for whoever looks after the team.                                                                                                                                              |
| **To work**                 | The hours your contract expects of you. Nothing ever reduces this figure — not leave, not a public holiday.                                                                                                           |
| **Worked**                  | The hours counted for you. Time on work items, plus hours with no work item, plus time off, plus public holidays.                                                                                                     |
| **Balance**                 | **Worked** minus **To work**. Positive means you are ahead, negative means you are behind.                                                                                                                            |
| **Running balance**         | On **Team time** only: the balance carried on from one month to the next. It is blank until a month is closed. On **My time** the same figure appears as a small line under **Balance** reading _"…carried forward"_. |
| **Work items**              | Hours that came from timers on work items.                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **No work item**            | The day-table column for hours you added by hand.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Hours with no work item** | The same hours, totalled for one day, in the day form.                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Time off**                | Absence and public holiday hours for that day, added together.                                                                                                                                                        |
| **When you were at work**   | The start, end, break and place for one day. A separate record. It does not count towards your hours.                                                                                                                 |
| **Starting balance**        | Where you stood on the day the system began counting for you. It has to be agreed by the person it belongs to, and agreeing cannot be taken back.                                                                     |
| **Hand in**                 | You say your month is complete.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Agree**                   | Somebody other than you says the figures are right.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Close**                   | The figures are frozen and go to payroll.                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Reopen**                  | A closed month put back so something in it can be corrected.                                                                                                                                                          |

### What the day column **What it was** can say

| Word                  | When                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Working day**       | An ordinary day you were meant to work.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Not a working day** | Nothing was scheduled for you that day — a weekend, or a weekday you do not work. **A public holiday falling on a day you never work says this too**, and credits you nothing. Nothing has been taken from you: you were not owed that day. |
| **Public holiday**    | A whole holiday on a day you would have worked.                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Half holiday**      | Half a day off for everybody. 24 and 31 December are usually half days.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Away**              | An absence covering the whole of that day.                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Partly away**       | An absence covering part of the day, or a half holiday with time off on top of it.                                                                                                                                                          |

### One warning about the downloaded file

The file you can download uses different words for the state of a month — Open, Submitted, Approved, Locked, Reopened — where the screen says **Not handed in**, **Handed in**, **Agreed**, **Closed** and **Reopened**. Only Locked in the file means the figures are final. The page "Team time — everybody’s month" says what to tell the accountant.

## How a length of time is written

**Everything is hours and minutes, written with a colon.**

`7:42` is seven hours and forty-two minutes. `161:42` is one hundred and sixty-one hours and forty-two minutes. These are lengths of time, not clock times, so the number in front of the colon goes past 24 and keeps counting. The number after the colon is always two digits and never goes above 59.

A balance always carries its sign, so that being level and being ahead cannot be confused.

| You see | It means                                                                                                                |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `+3:00` | Three hours ahead.                                                                                                      |
| `-1:06` | One hour and six minutes behind.                                                                                        |
| `0:00`  | Exactly level.                                                                                                          |
| `—`     | There is no figure at all. Not the same as zero. A dash in **Balance** on a day row means the day has not happened yet. |

**Decimal hours** appear in two places only: on the **Before you invoice** panel, for the people who invoice their own hours, and in the downloaded file. `7:42` written as decimal hours is `7.70`. To go from one to the other, divide the minutes by 60: 462 minutes ÷ 60 = 7.70 hours = 7:42.

### What you may type into each box

| Box                                               | What it accepts                                               | Watch out for                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **How long**, when adding hours to a day          | `1:30`, `1.5`, `7h`, `90m`                                    | A plain number on its own is refused, with _"Say how long it was: 1:30, 7h, or 90m. A number on its own does not say whether you mean hours or minutes."_ Type `20m` for twenty minutes, not `20`. |
| **Break (minutes)**, on **When you were at work** | A whole number of minutes                                     | `30` is half an hour. This is the one box in the system where a plain number means minutes.                                                                                                        |
| **Hours away each day**, on an absence            | `3:30`, `3.5`, `3h`, or `90m` for minutes                     | —                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| The days of the working week, on **People**       | `7:42`, or `7.7` for the same length written as decimal hours | Leave a day blank when it is not worked.                                                                                                                                                           |

Everything is held in whole minutes underneath. Nothing is ever worked out from a figure that has already been rounded for reading.

## A whole month, with the sums

Anna works five days a week, seven hours and forty-two minutes a day. She is paid a salary; she does not invoice her own hours.

Her working week is 5 × 7:42 = `38:30`.

Take a month with **21 working days** for her. One of those 21 is a public holiday. Two of them she takes as leave. That leaves 18 days she was actually at work.

Working in minutes, because that is what the system does — `7:42` is 462 minutes:

|                                      | Minutes | Shown as |
| ------------------------------------ | ------- | -------- |
| **To work** — 21 days × 462          | 9 702   | `161:42` |
| Timers on work items                 | 7 290   | `121:30` |
| Hours with no work item              | 960     | `16:00`  |
| Public holiday — 1 day × 462         | 462     | `7:42`   |
| Leave — 2 days × 462                 | 924     | `15:24`  |
| **Worked** — 7 290 + 960 + 462 + 924 | 9 636   | `160:36` |
| **Balance** — 9 636 − 9 702          | −66     | `-1:06`  |

The public holiday and the two days of leave are three of the 21 days. They still owe 7:42 each. That is why **To work** counts 21 days and not 18: the system adds those hours to **Worked** instead of taking them off **To work**.

Her month therefore reads:

> **To work** `161:42` · _38:30 a week_
>
> **Worked** `160:36` · _includes 23:06 away or on holiday_
>
> **Balance** `-1:06`

The `23:06` in that small line is the holiday and the leave together: 462 + 924 = 1 386 minutes. On the day rows themselves, each of those three days shows `7:42` in **To work**, `7:42` in **Time off**, `7:42` in **Worked** and `0:00` in **Balance**.

Anna ended the month an hour and six minutes short. That is what carries forward once the month is closed.

### The same month for somebody who invoices their own hours

Two of the four employment arrangements are self-invoicing: **Remote, full time, invoices us** and **Remote, part time, invoices us**. For those two, nothing is expected by the day, so:

- **To work** is `0:00` and **Balance** is `0:00`, in every month, for ever. That is correct, not a fault.
- A public holiday credits them nothing.
- Leave is not counted against a leave account.
- **Worked** is only the real hours: 7 290 + 960 = 8 250 minutes = `137:30`.

Those two people also get a panel called **Before you invoice** on their own month, with the hours in decimal form and what the month comes to at their rate. It says plainly whether the month is settled; while it is not, it reads _"This month is not closed yet, so these figures can still move. Wait until it is closed before you invoice."_

## The life of a month, from open to closed

A month moves in one direction. It can be put back, but only from the very end, and putting it back is recorded.

| Badge on screen   | What it means                                                                                       | Can the hours in it be changed? | What happens next                                                                 |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Not handed in** | Nobody has done anything yet. The figures are worked out again every time somebody opens the month. | Yes, by you                     | You press **Hand in**, once the month has ended                                   |
| **Handed in**     | You have said your month is complete. The figures stop being recalculated.                          | No                              | Somebody else presses **Agree**                                                   |
| **Agreed**        | Somebody other than you has said the figures are right.                                             | No                              | Whoever looks after the team presses **Close**                                    |
| **Closed**        | The figures are frozen. This is what payroll gets.                                                  | No                              | Nothing, unless it has to be corrected                                            |
| **Reopened**      | A closed month that was put back so something could be corrected.                                   | Yes                             | It is handed in, agreed and closed again. It never goes back to **Not handed in** |

### Handing in cannot be undone by you

Read this before you press it. Once you hand a month in, you cannot take it back, and you cannot change its hours, its time off or its attendance. There is no undo button anywhere.

The system asks first. The box says: _"Once {month} is handed in, you cannot change its hours, time off or attendance yourself. Ask whoever looks after the team if something needs correcting."_ **Cancel** leaves everything as it was.

If you find a mistake after handing in, tell whoever looks after the team. Correcting it means the month has to be agreed, then closed, then reopened — there is no shorter route. It is not a disaster, but it is four steps instead of none, so it is worth reading down your days before you press.

### What stops you handing in

The **Hand in** button is greyed out, or is not there at all, for one of three reasons.

- **The month has not ended.** Resting the pointer on the button says _"The month is not over yet. It can be handed in once it has ended."_ You can hand in from the first day of the following month.
- **A timer of yours is still running.** A running timer has no length yet, so any total including it would be wrong the moment it was read. A line under the figures says so: _"A timer is still running on one of your work items, in this or another workspace. Stop it, then you can hand the month in."_ Stop it on the work item.
- **The month has already been handed in.** Then the button is gone, and the badge beside the figures says what has become of the month.

### What the other steps do

**Agree** is somebody else saying your figures are right. It is one press, it cannot be undone, and nobody may agree their own month — the system refuses it outright. In a company this size that means whoever looks after the team needs a second person able to agree, or their own month can never be finished.

**Close** freezes the month. The figures stop moving. The hours, absences and attendance behind them stop accepting changes, and anything recorded into that month afterwards will simply never count. A month can only be closed once the month before it has been closed, so months are closed oldest first.

**Reopen** is the only way back, and it is only possible from **Closed**. Whoever reopens has to write down why, and both closings stay on the record.

## What you have to do, and when

Nothing reminds you. There are no emails, no notifications and no badges anywhere in this part of Plane. Your company sets its own deadline for handing a month in; ask whoever looks after the team what it is and put it in your calendar.

**During the month**

1. Work as usual, starting and stopping timers on work items. Those hours reach your month by themselves.
2. When you spend time on something with no work item behind it — a meeting, administration, training, travel — open **My time** and press **Record time for today**. The form for today opens. Add the time and press **Add**; a small message says **Added** and the entry appears in the list.
3. To add hours to a day that is not today, click the date in the day table instead. Any day of a month that is still open can be opened this way, including days that have not happened yet, and including months from before the company used this system.

**After the month has ended**

4. Open **My time** and step back to the month that has just finished, using the `‹` arrow beside the month name. The three figures change to the whole month's, and every day of the month is listed.
5. Read down the day table. Look for a working day with no hours on it that should have some, and for a **Balance** that does not look like your day.
6. Stop any timer of yours that is still running. The line under the figures tells you if there is one.
7. Press **Hand in**, read the box, and press **Hand in** again. A message appears: **Handed in** — _"{month} is now waiting to be agreed."_ The badge beside the figures changes to **Handed in**, and the button goes.

That is the whole of your part. Whoever looks after the team does the rest.

## Figures that look wrong and are not

**Time off and public holidays are added to Worked, not taken off To work.** A week of leave shows a full week of hours nobody spent at a desk. This is on purpose: it keeps the hours column handed to payroll equal to the hours you are actually paid for, and the balance comes out exactly the same either way. Wherever it applies, a small line under **Worked** says _"includes … away or on holiday"_.

**A public holiday on a day you never work shows as Not a working day and adds nothing.** A holiday is only ever worth what you would have worked that day. If you never work Mondays, a Monday holiday is worth nothing to you — and nothing has been taken from you, because you were never owed those hours.

**On the day form, the two totals are not supposed to match.** The figure beside **When you were at work** is how long you were at work. The list underneath it is only the hours no work item accounts for. Your work-item hours are counted towards the month but are not shown in that list at all. Nobody has to make those two figures agree, and nobody should try.

**Running balance is blank until a month is closed.** It is written at the moment of closing and not before, so a dash there means "not closed yet", not "zero".

**Merely looking at a month creates it.** Stepping to a month nobody has opened before writes the record for it, including months in the future and months from before you were hired. An empty month is not evidence that hours went missing; check when the person started.

**An entry cannot be edited.** To correct hours you added by hand, remove the row and add it again. That is possible for as long as the month is still open.

**Figures do not always move the instant something changes.** Recording or agreeing an absence, or bringing records in from a file, does not move anybody's monthly figures straight away. A month is worked out again the next time somebody opens it, and by itself, every hour, for the current month and the one before.

**An hour counts on the day its timer started.** A timer begun at 23:40 on the last day of a month counts wholly in that month, not in the next one.
