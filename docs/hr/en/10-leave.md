# Leave

This page is for anybody who wants to know how much leave they have, and for whoever writes that figure down for the team. It explains where the number comes from, why it is kept in hours and minutes but shown in days, why the leave year probably does not start in January, what one day of leave is actually worth, how leave is carried over from one year to the next, and what happens to a month when somebody takes time off.

Booking time off, and agreeing or refusing it, is on another page: "Time away from work".

## Where leave is written down, and where you read it off

Leave lives in three separate places, and they answer three different questions.

| The question                                                  | Where the answer is                                                       | Who can see it                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| How much leave was granted for this leave year                | The **Leave** window, opened from **People**                              | Whoever looks after the team                                          |
| How much leave is left today                                  | The line under the three figures on **My time**: "3.5 days of leave left" | Only the person themselves                                            |
| Which days somebody is away, and whether that has been agreed | **Absences**                                                              | The person, for their own; whoever looks after the team, for everyone |

There is no screen anywhere that shows one person how much leave a colleague has left. The figure appears only on that person's own **My time** page. If you look after the team and somebody asks you how much leave they have, the answer is either on their screen or worked out by hand from the granted figure and the days they have taken.

To reach the **Leave** window:

1. Open **My time** from the left sidebar.
2. Press the "…" button at the top right and choose **Everyone's hours**. This item is only there for whoever looks after the team.
3. In the quiet row of links beside the month, press **People**.
4. On the person's row, press the "…" button at the right and choose **Leave**.

The window opens with that person's name in the title, for example "Anna Berger's leave". Under the title it says:

> Leave is counted in hours, not days, because a working day is not the same length for everyone. The leave year runs from the anniversary of joining.

If nothing has been recorded for that person yet, the window says "No leave year on record yet."

## Why leave is held in hours and minutes

Every figure in this system is stored as a whole number of minutes. That includes leave.

The reason is that a day is not the same length for everybody, and it is not even the same length for one person across a year. Somebody working Monday to Thursday full days and a short Friday does not spend the same amount of time on a Friday off as on a Monday off. If contracted hours change in the middle of a year, a figure written down in days stops meaning one thing.

So the quantity that is kept is hours and minutes. Days are worked out for display, from the working week that applies at the moment you are looking, and are never stored.

That is why the box you type the year's leave into is labelled **Leave for the year (hours:minutes)** and its example reads "e.g. 200:00".

Three ways of typing a length are accepted in that box:

| What you type | What is stored | Note                                             |
| ------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| `200:00`      | 200 hours      | Hours, a colon, then minutes. The clearest form. |
| `200`         | 200 hours      | A bare number is read as **hours**, not minutes. |
| `12000m`      | 200 hours      | Minutes, with an `m` on the end.                 |

If the box holds something that cannot be read, the window refuses to save and says: "Write the time as 200:00, 200 or 12000m."

As soon as what you have typed can be read, a small grey line appears under the box showing it back to you, for example "200:00 · about 26.0 days". Read that line before you press **Add**. It is there so that a figure typed in the wrong unit is caught now rather than in March, when somebody runs out of leave.

## What a day of leave is worth

A day, for the purpose of turning hours into days, is the **average of the days that person is actually scheduled to work**. Days they do not work are left out of the average.

Two examples.

**Daniel** works Monday to Thursday 8:00 and Friday 6:30. His week is 38:30.

```
Mon 8:00 + Tue 8:00 + Wed 8:00 + Thu 8:00 + Fri 6:30 = 38:30 a week
38:30 = 2 310 minutes, over 5 working days
2 310 ÷ 5 = 462 minutes = 7:42 — one "day" for Daniel
```

So Daniel's 200:00 of leave reads as "about 26.0 days". But no single day off actually costs him 7:42. A Friday off costs him 6:30; a Monday off costs him 8:00. The day figure is a conversion for reading, not a price list.

**Sofia** works Monday to Thursday 7:42 and does not work Fridays. Her week is 30:48.

```
30:48 = 1 848 minutes, over 4 working days
1 848 ÷ 4 = 462 minutes = 7:42 — one "day" for Sofia
```

Sofia booking a whole week off, Monday to Friday, spends four days of leave, not five. The Friday costs her nothing, because she does not work it.

Under a flexitime agreement, where the daily distribution is the person's own to choose, a day is instead the notional day that agreement names. There is no "what they would have worked" to read off a schedule in that case.

**Where no day length can be worked out**, no day figure is shown at all, and the hours are shown on their own instead. This happens in two places:

- On **My time**, the leave line reads "192:51 of leave left" rather than "25.0 days of leave left".
- In the **Leave** window on **People**, the "about … days" wording is simply missing from the row. That window reads the person's **own** working week only. If their row on **People** shows **Not set up** in the **Hours a week** column, they have no working week of their own, and the **Leave** window can show hours but not days.

A day count guessed from nothing would be worse than no day count, which is why nothing is shown rather than a wrong figure.

## The leave year

The leave year runs from the **anniversary of joining**, not from January. That is the statutory default here.

So somebody who started on 1 March 2024 has a leave year running 1 March to 28 February, and their leave resets on 1 March, not on 1 January. Somebody who started on 15 September has a leave year running 15 September to 14 September.

Three things follow from this, and all three catch people out.

**The dates are typed by hand.** Nothing works the anniversary out for you. When you record a leave year you fill in **Leave year from** and **Leave year to** yourself. Calendar-year dates are the natural guess for anyone who has done payroll before, and nothing on the screen stops you entering them. Look at the person's **Since** date on **People** before you type.

**If no leave year covers today, the leave figure disappears.** The person's **My time** page simply stops showing the leave line. There is no message and no warning anywhere saying why. If somebody tells you their leave has vanished from their screen, the first thing to check is whether a leave year has been recorded that covers today's date.

**The figure on My time is always the leave year covering today.** Stepping back to look at March of last year does not show last year's leave. The leave line always answers "how much do I have now", whichever month is on screen.

## Recording a leave year

Read this before you start: **a leave year cannot be corrected or removed once it has been added.** There is no edit control and no delete on the screen, and a second leave year covering the same dates is refused. Check the figures before you press **Add**.

1. Open the **Leave** window for the person, as described above. Any leave years already recorded are listed, newest first.
2. In the box under **Add a leave year**, fill in **Leave year from** and **Leave year to**. These are date boxes. Both are required.
3. Fill in **Leave for the year (hours:minutes)**. Check the small grey line that appears under it, which reads the figure back to you in days.
4. Fill in **Carried over** if there is any. It is optional. See the next section.
5. Fill in **What this is based on** if you want to. It is optional, and it is where you say where the figure came from — a contract clause, a letter, the old spreadsheet.
6. Press **Add**. A green message says "Leave year added", the new row appears in the list above, and the three text boxes empty. The two dates stay as they are, so a second person's year is not what you are being offered next.

If something is missing or does not read, a red line appears above the button and nothing is sent:

| What the red line says                                | What is wrong                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Say when the leave year starts and ends."            | One of the two date boxes is empty.                                               |
| "The leave year cannot end before it starts."         | The end date is earlier than the start date.                                      |
| "Write the time as 200:00, 200 or 12000m."            | The leave figure, or the carried-over figure, cannot be read as a length of time. |
| "There is already a leave year covering these dates." | A leave year already on the list overlaps the dates you typed, even by one day.   |

That last one is worth understanding. It is not only the same first day that is refused — any overlap at all is. A leave year laid over an existing one would win wherever it starts later, so the carry-over in the older row would quietly stop applying and everything taken since the anniversary would be charged against a year nobody meant to open.

If the save is refused by the system rather than by the form, a red message appears in the corner headed "That could not be saved", with the reason underneath, or "Please try again." where no reason could be read.

### What each row in the list shows

| Part of the row                                          | What it is                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `208:15`                                                 | The whole amount granted for that year: the year's leave plus anything carried over. |
| "about 27.0 days"                                        | The same amount in days, if a day length is known.                                   |
| "1 Mar 2026 to 28 Feb 2027"                              | The leave year.                                                                      |
| "8:15 carried over"                                      | Shown only when something was carried over.                                          |
| **Hold as final**, or a green tick and **Held as final** | Whether the figure has been frozen. See below.                                       |

### Holding a figure as final

**Hold as final** freezes the figure. Once held, it cannot be changed on any screen ever again — the system refuses the change with the sentence "This entitlement has been agreed with the person. Record a further adjustment rather than editing what was agreed."

Because a leave year cannot be edited from this window in the first place, holding it as final is mainly a mark that the figure has been settled with the person and checked.

Pressing **Hold as final** asks first. The question is "Hold this figure as final?" and the body reads:

> 208:15 can no longer be changed on any screen afterwards. Check it against what the person was told before you hold it.

Press **Hold as final** again to confirm, or **Cancel**. A green message says "Held as final" and the row changes to a green tick and the words **Held as final**.

## Carrying leave over

Leave left at the end of a leave year is **not** carried into the next one by itself. Nothing moves it. If you record next year without a carry-over, next year starts at the year's figure alone, and whatever was left of the old year stops being counted from the anniversary onwards.

Carrying leave over is one number, typed by hand, into the **Carried over** box when you add the new leave year. Its placeholder says: "Optional. Use a minus if they have already taken some of it."

- A **positive** figure is leave brought forward from the year before. Type it as `8:15` for eight hours and fifteen minutes.
- A **minus** figure is leave taken in advance — somebody who has already had some of this year's leave before the year began. Type it as `-8:15`.
- Leaving it empty means nothing is carried over.

The carried-over figure is added into the total for the year. In the list, a year with 200:00 of leave and 8:15 carried over shows as `208:15` with the chip "8:15 carried over" beside it.

**Write it with a colon.** This box has no read-back line under it, unlike the leave figure above it. `8:15` means eight hours and fifteen minutes. `8.15` is read as decimal hours and comes out as 8:09 — six minutes short, with no complaint from anywhere.

There is no rule in the system about how much may be carried over, or by when it must be used. That is a decision somebody makes and then types in.

## How much leave is left

The figure people actually want is on their own **My time** page, in the small grey line under the three headline figures, next to a palm-tree icon: "25.0 days of leave left".

It is worked out like this:

```
what is left = everything granted for this leave year
             − every day of leave already agreed anywhere in that leave year
```

Four things about that sum are worth knowing.

**Only agreed leave is counted.** A request that is still showing as **Asked for** has not been taken off anybody's leave. Neither has one that was **Refused** or **Withdrawn**. Until whoever looks after the team presses **Agree** on it, the leave is still shown as available.

**Leave booked for later in the year has already been taken off.** The sum covers the whole leave year, not the part of it that has happened. Somebody who has booked two weeks in August sees those two weeks gone from the figure in March. This is deliberate: the figure answers "how much may I still book", not "how much have I used so far".

**It can go below zero.** Nothing stops somebody being granted more leave than they have. The figure is signed, so leave taken in advance shows as a negative rather than as zero.

**A public holiday inside a booked week costs no leave**, and a day off sick during booked leave takes the day instead of the leave. Both are handled in the arithmetic, so the figure on **My time** is the one to trust. See the next section.

The figure is always for the leave year covering today, and it is only ever your own.

## How taking leave shows up in a month

The most important rule in the whole system, and the one nobody is ever told: **time off does not reduce the hours you are expected to work. It is added to the hours you have worked instead.**

So a week of leave shows up as a full week of **Worked** hours that nobody spent at a desk. The **To work** figure does not move. The **Balance** comes out the same either way, and the hours column that goes to the payroll accountant then contains the hours the person is actually paid for.

The only sign of this on screen is the small hint under the **Worked** figure: "includes 15:24 away or on holiday".

### What a day of leave looks like in the day table

On **My time**, a day of agreed leave reads like this:

| Column           | What it shows on a day of leave                                                                        |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **What it was**  | **Away** when the leave covers the whole scheduled day, **Partly away** when it covers only part of it |
| **To work**      | The full hours for that weekday, unchanged                                                             |
| **Work items**   | Whatever was logged on work items, usually 0:00                                                        |
| **No work item** | Whatever was added by hand, usually 0:00                                                               |
| **Time off**     | The hours the leave credits                                                                            |
| **Worked**       | The same as **To work**, because the leave has filled it                                               |
| **Balance**      | 0:00                                                                                                   |

A day of leave on a day the person does not work reads **Not a working day** and credits nothing. That is correct, and no leave is spent for it.

### A worked example

**Anna** works Monday to Friday, 7:42 a day. That is 462 minutes a day and 38:30 a week. Her leave year runs 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027, with 200:00 granted and 8:15 carried over — so `208:15` in total, about 27.0 days.

The month has **21 working days**, one of which is a **public holiday**, and Anna takes **two days of leave**.

```
To work        21 × 462                        = 9 702 minutes = 161:42
```

Nothing below changes that figure.

```
Work items                                     = 8 196 minutes = 136:36
No work item (two internal meetings)           =   120 minutes =   2:00
Time off: public holiday          1 × 462      =   462 minutes =   7:42
          leave                   2 × 462      =   924 minutes =  15:24
                                                 -----------------------
Worked    8 196 + 120 + 462 + 924              = 9 702 minutes = 161:42
Balance   9 702 − 9 702                        =     0 minutes =   0:00
```

Her month comes out exactly level. The two days away and the public holiday were credited; nothing was taken off what she owed.

What it cost her leave:

```
Leave spent    2 × 462                         =   924 minutes =  15:24 = 2.0 days
Granted        200:00 + 8:15                   = 12 495 minutes
Left           12 495 − 924                    = 11 571 minutes = 192:51 ≈ 25.0 days
```

The public holiday cost her no leave at all. She did not book it, and it was credited before any leave was.

### A public holiday inside a booked week

This is the one place where two figures on two screens legitimately disagree, and it looks like a fault.

Anna books Monday to Friday off as annual leave, and the Thursday of that week is a public holiday.

On **Absences**, the **Hours away** column reads `38:30`. That column says how long the absence runs against her working week, and it does not know about public holidays.

In the month, the holiday takes the Thursday first, and only what is left is charged to her leave:

```
Public holiday, Thursday          1 × 462      =   462 minutes =   7:42
Leave, the other four days        4 × 462      = 1 848 minutes =  30:48
```

So the row says 38:30 and her leave account is charged 30:48. Both figures are right for what they measure. **Read what leave was actually spent from the leave figure on My time, not from the Absences column.**

### When the figures actually move

Recording or agreeing an absence does not recalculate anybody's month straight away. The month is worked out again the next time somebody opens it, or when **Bring up to date** is pressed on it, or when it is handed in or closed, or when the scheduled hourly job picks it up — which covers the current month and the one before it.

So after agreeing somebody's leave, expect their figures to be right the next time that month is opened, not the instant you pressed **Agree**.

Once a month has been closed, its figures are frozen. Leave recorded into a closed month afterwards is refused outright, and would not reach the figures even if it got in.

For the record that goes to the payroll accountant, the downloaded month file carries a `leave_consumed_minutes` column: the minutes of leave that month used up, per person.

## People who have no leave account

Two of the four employment arrangements are for people who invoice us for their own hours. For them, no leave is counted at all.

This follows one switch on **People**, in the person's own window, under **Terms**:

> **Expect set hours each day, keep a running balance, and count the leave they take**

with the note under it:

> Leave this on for employees. Turn it off for anyone who invoices us for their own hours: no hours are expected of them each day, no balance is kept and no leave is counted.

When that switch is off:

- No leave line appears on that person's **My time** page.
- An absence recorded as **Annual leave** for them credits nothing to the month and spends nothing from any leave account. It is neither counted as worked nor charged to leave.
- The **Leave** window will still happily accept a leave year for them, and it will sit there being counted by nothing.

If somebody's leave figure never appears, or an agreed week of leave makes no difference at all to their month, this switch is the thing to check first.

One related trap. On the **Starting balance** window there is a **What for** choice offering **Hours over or under**, **Leave** and **Overtime to be paid**. Only **Hours over or under** is read by anything. A starting balance recorded as **Leave** is stored and displayed and counted by nothing. Leave belongs in the **Leave** window, not there.

## Things that cannot be taken back

Say these out loud before pressing anything.

| Action                  | Why it cannot be undone                                                                                               |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adding a leave year     | There is no way to edit or delete it from any screen, and an overlapping second one is refused. A wrong figure stays. |
| **Hold as final**       | The figure is frozen for good. Later changes have to be recorded separately, with their own reason.                   |
| Not carrying leave over | Nothing moves it for you. Once the new leave year is running, the old year's remainder is no longer counted.          |

Nothing in the system tells anybody about any of this. No message is sent when a leave year is recorded, when a request for time off is waiting, when it is agreed, or when it is refused. Those conversations happen in words, as they always did.
