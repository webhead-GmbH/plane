# My time — your own month

This is the page every employee uses. It shows your own working month: how many hours you owe, how many you have put in, and whether you are ahead or behind. It is also where you do the one thing you have to do each month. This page of the handbook explains every figure on it, where each figure comes from, and what to do before the month ends.

Nobody else's hours appear here, and you cannot look at anybody else's month from it.

## Getting to your month

1. In the sidebar down the left of the screen, find **My time**. It has a small clock beside it.
2. Press it. Your own month opens, and the month it opens on is the one running now.

The page is always about you. It does not matter which part of the product you were in when you pressed it, and it does not matter which workspace the address in your browser names. Hours you record in any workspace of the company count towards the same month here.

## Moving between months

The month you are looking at is named at the top left, as a word and a year — for example, `March 2026`. Either side of it is a small arrow.

- The left arrow steps back one month.
- The right arrow steps forward one month.

Neither arrow ever stops. You can step back to months from before you were employed and forward to months that have not happened. Both look like ordinary months with no hours in them, because that is what they are. A month full of zeros means nothing was recorded in it — it does not mean anything is broken.

## The three figures at the top

Three cards sit across the top of the page. Everything else on the page is there to explain them.

They answer two different questions depending on where the month is:

- **While the month is still running**, they count only the days that have happened, up to and including today. Under the first card you then see how big the whole month is going to be.
- **Once the month has ended**, they count the whole month.

The small line under the figures tells you which of the two you are reading. It is described in [the next section](#the-notes-under-the-figures).

### To work

What your contract asks of you.

It is the sum of the hours set for each of your days: your working week says how long a Monday is, how long a Tuesday is, and so on, and this adds those up over the month.

Three things are worth knowing about it.

- **Nothing takes hours off it.** Not a public holiday, not a day of leave, not a day of sickness. Those are added to **Worked** instead, which is explained below. The reason is that the hours sent to payroll then contain the hours you are actually paid for.
- **It is worked out day by day.** If your terms or your working week changed in the middle of the month, the days before the change use the old figure and the days after it use the new one.
- **It is 0:00 for anyone who invoices us for their own hours.** No set hours are expected of them each day, so there is nothing for them to be above or below.

Under the figure you see one of two small lines:

| The line                          | When it appears                  | What it means                                                                 |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **of 161:42 for the whole month** | While the month is still running | The big figure counts the days so far. This is what the whole month comes to. |
| **38:30 a week**                  | Once the month has ended         | Your contracted working week, as a reminder of where the figure came from.    |

### Worked

The hours that count as worked. It is four things added together:

1. Hours from timers on work items.
2. Hours you entered here with no work item behind them.
3. Time off that is paid — leave, sickness, and so on.
4. Public holidays.

Points 3 and 4 surprise people, so they are worth saying plainly. **Time off and public holidays are added to your worked hours rather than taken off the hours you were expected to work.** A week of leave therefore shows a full week of hours nobody spent at a desk. Your over-or-under figure still comes out right, because both sides of the sum are left alone.

When any of your worked hours are time off, a small line appears under the figure:

- **includes 23:06 away or on holiday**

That line is the only sign on the page that part of the figure was not time at a desk. If it is not there, none of the figure is time off.

### Balance

**Worked** minus **To work**. It is the figure the page exists for.

- Green with a plus in front when you are ahead: `+7:30`.
- Red, on a pink card, when you are behind: `-8:12`.
- Grey `0:00` when you are level.

It is always `0:00` for anyone who invoices us for their own hours. No hours are expected of them, so there is no surplus and no shortfall.

Under the figure you see one of these:

| The line                          | When it appears           | What it means                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **5:06 behind so far this month** | The balance is negative   | The same figure again, in words, without the minus sign.                                                                                        |
| **3:00 ahead so far this month**  | The balance is positive   | The same.                                                                                                                                       |
| **-3:06 carried forward**         | The month has been closed | Your running total after this month — what you were carrying into it, plus this month's balance. This is the figure the next month starts from. |

Two notes on the carried-forward figure.

Some kinds of time off are taken as hours rather than paid — time off in lieu. A day like that is credited to **Worked**, and the same number of minutes comes off the running total when the month is closed. That is why the carried-forward figure is not always the opening balance plus the month's balance.

If a month that was closed has since been reopened, the carried-forward line still shows the figure from when it was closed, while the three cards above show the month as it stands now. The two can disagree. Read the cards; the line will be worked out again when the month is closed a second time.

## The notes under the figures

Under the three cards is one line of small grey notes. Each one appears only when it applies, in this order.

**How far the figures reach.** One of:

- **As things stand on Tue 17 Mar. The month is not over.**
- **This month has not started yet.**

If neither is there, the month has ended and the figures cover all of it.

**Where the month stands.** A small badge with a clock, always shown. It is one of five:

| Badge             | What it means                                                                                                     |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Not handed in** | The month is yours. You can still change hours, time off and attendance in it.                                    |
| **Handed in**     | You have handed it in. It is waiting for somebody to agree it. You can no longer change anything in it.           |
| **Agreed**        | Somebody has agreed it. It is waiting to be closed.                                                               |
| **Closed**        | The figures are final. The badge turns grey.                                                                      |
| **Reopened**      | It had been closed and was put back into play so something could be corrected. You can change things in it again. |

The whole way through that sequence is described on the handbook page "The month, from open to closed".

**A timer left running.**

- **A timer is still running on one of your work items, in this or another workspace. Stop it, then you can hand the month in.**

A running timer has no length yet, so any total including it would be out of date the moment you read it. This is why the month cannot be handed in while one is going. Stop it on the work item it belongs to. It counts as running in this month if it started in this month, wherever in the company you started it.

**Leave left.**

- **17.0 days of leave left**, or **131:24 of leave left** where a day cannot be given a length.

Four things about this figure, none of which fit on the screen:

- Leave is held in hours and minutes and shown to you in days. A day means the average of the days you are actually scheduled to work — so if your days are uneven, the day used here is a length you never actually work. Under a flexitime agreement it is the notional day that agreement names.
- The leave year runs from the anniversary of the day you started, not from January.
- Leave you have already booked for later in that leave year has already been taken off. The figure is what is left after everything you have arranged, not what is left as of today.
- Only time off that has been agreed counts. A request still waiting on somebody changes nothing at all — not your month, not this figure.
- It can go negative if you have been allowed leave in advance.

**Days worked from home.**

- **From home this year: 24**

A count for the calendar year, of days whose attendance record says you worked from home. It only appears for people whose attendance is kept at all.

**Days that need looking at.**

- **Some days need looking at — hours that were counted before are no longer there. Whoever looks after the team has to settle them before the month can be closed.**

This appears when hours a day had counted earlier are no longer anywhere in the month — most often because a work item was deleted, which takes every timer on it. Nothing on your page can clear this. Whoever looks after the team settles it, with a written note, from the team page.

## Before you invoice

This block appears only for the two arrangements where you invoice us for your own hours. Everybody else never sees it.

It reads: **Before you invoice** — _What this month comes to at your rate, so the figure is agreed before the invoice is written rather than argued about after it arrives._

| Field        | What it holds                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Hours**    | The month's worked hours, as `h:mm` — for example `154:30` — with **154.50 in decimal hours** underneath. The decimal form is what goes on an invoice. |
| **Rate**     | Either **65.00 EUR an hour** or **A fixed monthly amount**. It is the rate that was in force on the first day of the month.                            |
| **Comes to** | The hours at that rate, to the cent.                                                                                                                   |

Two things to know before you write the invoice.

**The Hours figure is the same figure as the Worked card**, so any paid time off you had in the month is inside it. Public holidays are not — a public holiday credits nothing to somebody who invoices their own hours. If you had paid time off in the month, check the **includes … away or on holiday** line on the Worked card and decide with whoever looks after the team whether those hours belong on the invoice.

**Wait for the month to be closed.** The last line of the block says which it is:

- **This month is not closed yet, so these figures can still move. Wait until it is closed before you invoice.**
- **The month is closed, so these figures are settled.**

If no rate has been recorded for you, the block shows the hours and this instead: **No rate on record for you yet, so this shows the hours only. Ask whoever looks after the team.**

If you expect this block and it is not there at all, reload the page before concluding anything about your arrangement.

## The day table, column by column

Below everything else is one row for every day of the month. Eight columns, left to right.

| Column           | What is in it                                                                                                             |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Day**          | The date, as `Mon 2 Mar`. It is a button — pressing it opens that day. A small pencil appears when you move over the row. |
| **What it was**  | What kind of day it was. The six possible answers are below.                                                              |
| **To work**      | The hours set for that day by your working week.                                                                          |
| **Work items**   | Hours from timers on work items on that day.                                                                              |
| **No work item** | Hours you entered on that day with no work item behind them.                                                              |
| **Time off**     | Paid time off plus public holiday, added together.                                                                        |
| **Worked**       | **Work items** plus **No work item** plus **Time off**. Shown in bold, because it is the day's answer.                    |
| **Balance**      | **Worked** minus **To work**, in green or red.                                                                            |

The six answers in **What it was**:

| Label                 | When you see it                                                                      |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Working day**       | An ordinary day you were due to work.                                                |
| **Not a working day** | Nothing was scheduled for you on that weekday.                                       |
| **Public holiday**    | A whole-day public holiday on a day you would have worked.                           |
| **Half holiday**      | A part-day public holiday. The 24th and 31st of December are commonly half days.     |
| **Away**              | Time off covering the whole of that day.                                             |
| **Partly away**       | Time off covering part of the day, or a part-day holiday with time off on top of it. |

Three things this table does that catch people out.

**A public holiday on a day you never work reads as "Not a working day", and credits 0:00.** The kind of day is decided by your working week first. Somebody who never works Mondays sees a Monday public holiday as an ordinary non-working day. Nothing has been taken from you: a public holiday is only ever worth what you would have worked that day, and on that day you would have worked nothing. For the same reason a public holiday is worth a different number of hours to each person, and worth nothing at all to anyone who invoices their own hours.

**Rows for days nobody was expected to work are dimmed, never hidden.** Somebody looking for where their hours went needs to see that a gap was a Saturday and not a day they forgot.

**The table says nothing about whether you recorded when you were at work.** If your terms include keeping start and end times, that record is kept day by day and the only way to see whether a day has one is to open the day. See the handbook page "When you were at work".

An amber warning triangle beside the label in **What it was** means: _Hours counted here earlier are no longer there._ This is the same thing the note under the figures warns about. You cannot clear it.

Today's row is tinted and its date is shown in bold colour.

### Days still to come

While the month is still running, the days that have not happened yet are left out of the table. Underneath it you get a button:

- **Show the rest of the month (14 days)** — press it and they appear.
- **Hide the rest of the month** — press it again and they go away.

Their **Balance** shows as `—`, and hovering it says _This day has not happened yet._

Once the month has ended there are no days still to come, so the button is not there and every row is shown.

## Opening a day

Pressing a date opens that day. Two things live inside:

- Where you were at work, if your terms include keeping start and end times. See "When you were at work".
- The hours on that day that no work item accounts for — meetings, admin, training, travel. See "Recording hours".

Both are covered on their own pages, but three points belong here because they are about reading this screen.

**The two records are not meant to agree.** The figure at the top of the day is when you were at work. The list below it is only the time no work item accounts for. The hours from your timers make up the rest and are shown nowhere in that dialog. A gap between the two is normal and nobody has to close it.

**Hours can go in on any day of a month that is still open**, including days later this month and months from before the company used this system. That is deliberate; it is how old months get filled in.

**The button at the top of the page always opens today.** **Record time for today** jumps the whole page to the current month and opens today's day, whatever month you were looking at when you pressed it. It reads today from your own computer's clock. To put hours on any other day, step to the right month and press that date in the table.

Time off is not recorded here. There is nothing in the day for being away, and the list of things you can choose from does not include it. Do not book a day of sickness or leave as **Administration** — it would count as time worked, it would never touch your leave, and it would reach payroll as admin work. Time off is recorded on the Absences page; see "Time away from work".

Recording or agreeing time off does not move your figures straight away. The month is worked out again the next time somebody opens it.

## The menu behind the three dots

To the right of the buttons at the top is a small button with three dots. It holds the occasional things.

| Item                  | What it does                                                                                             |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Starting balance**  | Opens your starting balance. Described below.                                                            |
| **Download as CSV**   | Downloads this month, one row per day, as a spreadsheet file. It is named after the month and after you. |
| **Download as Excel** | The same file as an Excel workbook.                                                                      |
| **Bring up to date**  | Works the month out again. Only offered while the month can still be changed.                            |
| **Hours in detail**   | Your own hours, entry by entry, for the month. See "Hours in detail".                                    |
| **Everyone’s hours**  | Only for whoever looks after the team. See "Team time — everybody’s month".                              |

Two notes.

**Bring up to date rarely changes anything.** The month is already worked out again every time you open this page. The item is there for the rare case where you want to be certain. If the figures look wrong, pressing it is unlikely to be the answer.

**The downloaded file counts in minutes, not in hours and minutes.** A day of `462` in the file is the `7:42` you see on screen.

### Starting balance

Opening **Starting balance** shows where you stood on the day counting started here. There is no history in the system from before then, so these figures are agreed with you rather than worked out — which is why each one records what it was based on and how sure anyone is of it.

Each row shows the amount, what it is for (**Hours over or under**, **Leave**, or **Overtime to be paid**), the date it applies from, a chip saying how sure anyone is (**Taken from records**, **Worked out from other records**, **Estimated**, or **From a conversation with them**), and what it was based on.

At the right of a row you get one of two things:

- **Agreed**, with a green tick, if you have already agreed it.
- A button, **This is right**.

**Agreeing cannot be taken back.** There is no way to undo it anywhere in the product. The confirmation says so: _You are agreeing that -12:30 is right. This cannot be taken back. If the figure is wrong, do not agree to it — say so to whoever looks after the team._ If you are not sure, close the dialog and ask first.

If nothing has been recorded, you see: _No starting balance has been recorded yet, so counting begins from zero._

You cannot record or correct a balance from this page. That is done by whoever looks after the team, on the People page.

Press **Done** to close.

## What you must do before the month ends

This is the whole of your monthly routine.

### Through the month

1. Let the timers on work items do their work. Those hours arrive here on their own.
2. Add anything the timers do not cover — meetings, admin, training, travel. Open the day and put them in.
3. Get any time off recorded and agreed. Time off that is still waiting on somebody counts for nothing, and your month will be short by exactly that much.

To correct a line of hours you entered, remove it and enter it again. There is no way to edit one in place. You can do that for as long as the month has not been handed in.

### Once the month has ended

1. Open **My time** and step to the month that has just finished, if the page is not already on it.
2. Read the three figures. The **Balance** should be a figure you can account for.
3. Look down the **Balance** column for any day that surprises you, and open that day.
4. Check there is no note about a timer still running. If there is, stop the timer on its work item and come back.
5. Press **Hand in**.

### Handing in

**Hand in** is the blue button at the top right. It only works when all three of these are true:

- The month has ended. You cannot hand in a month that is still running.
- No timer of yours is still going anywhere in that month.
- The month has not already been handed in.

While the button is greyed out, hovering it tells you why:

- **The month is not over yet. It can be handed in once it has ended.**
- **A timer is still running on one of your work items, in this or another workspace. Stop it, then hand the month in.**

**Handing in cannot be undone by you.** Pressing the button asks first:

> **Hand in** — Once March 2026 is handed in, you cannot change its hours, time off or attendance yourself. Ask whoever looks after the team if something needs correcting.

Press **Hand in** again to confirm, or **Cancel** to go back.

### After you have handed in

- A message appears: **Handed in** — _March 2026 is now waiting to be agreed._
- The badge under the figures changes to **Handed in**.
- The **Hand in** button disappears. There is nothing left for you to press.
- Opening any day of that month shows what is recorded but no longer lets you change it: _This month is no longer open, so its hours cannot be changed. Ask whoever looks after the team to reopen it if something needs correcting._

If you find a mistake after handing in, say so to whoever looks after the team. They can put the month back into play, and then you can correct it and hand it in again.

**The page carries no deadline.** It will not remind you and it will not chase you. Ask whoever looks after the team by which day of the following month your hours have to be in, and write it down.

## A month worked through, with the sums

Anna is on site, full time. Her working week is Monday to Friday, 7:42 a day — seven hours and forty-two minutes — which is 38:30 a week.

Take a month in which she is due to work on 21 days. One of those days is a public holiday. She takes two days of leave. On the remaining 18 days she works.

### What she owes

    21 days x 7:42  =  161:42

That is the **To work** figure. The public holiday does not reduce it. The two days of leave do not reduce it.

### What she is credited

    Public holiday        7:42
    Two days of leave    15:24   (2 x 7:42)
    ------------------------------
    Time off, in total   23:06

Those 23:06 are what the **Time off** column adds up to over the month, and they are inside her **Worked** figure.

### What she actually put in

On her 18 working days she logged 129:00 on work items and added 4:30 of meetings and admin with no work item behind them.

    Work items          129:00
    No work item          4:30
    Time off             23:06
    ------------------------------
    Worked              156:36

### Where that leaves her

    Worked              156:36
    To work            -161:42
    ------------------------------
    Balance              -5:06

The card is red and the line under it reads **5:06 behind so far this month**.

That shortfall is exactly what she did not put in on the 18 days she worked. Those days owed 18 × 7:42 = 138:36, and she recorded 133:30 against them. The difference is 5:06. The public holiday and the two days of leave are not part of it: each of them credited exactly what it owed, so each of those days came out at 0:00.

### Mid-month

If Anna opens this same month on a day when 11 of her 21 days have gone, the top of the page reads:

> **To work** 84:42 — _of 161:42 for the whole month_
> **Worked** 92:24 — _includes 15:24 away or on holiday_
> **Balance** +7:42 — _7:42 ahead so far this month_
>
> As things stand on Tue 17 Mar. The month is not over. · **Not handed in** · 17.0 days of leave left

84:42 is 11 × 7:42. **Hand in** is greyed out, and hovering it says _The month is not over yet. It can be handed in once it has ended._

### After it is closed

Anna carried +2:00 into this month from the month before. When the month is agreed and closed:

    Carried in           +2:00
    This month's balance -5:06
    ------------------------------
    Carried forward      -3:06

The badge turns grey and reads **Closed**, the three figures stop moving, and the line under **Balance** reads **-3:06 carried forward**. That is what next month starts from.

### Two people the same month treats differently

Daniel invoices us for his own hours. His **To work** is 0:00 and his **Balance** is 0:00, in every month. The public holiday credits him nothing. The block he reads is **Before you invoice**.

Sofia is part time and does not work Mondays. The public holiday in this month falls on a Monday. Her row for it reads **Not a working day**, with 0:00 in every column. Nothing was taken from her; she was not due to work that day.

## When the page shows something else

**No employment record here** — _Your hours are not being tracked yet. If they should be, ask whoever looks after the team to set you up._

You have an account, but nobody has set up an employment record for you. Nothing on this page will work until they do.

**This could not be loaded** — _Something went wrong on the way. Try again, and tell whoever looks after the team if it keeps happening._

The page could not fetch your month. Press **Try again**. If it keeps happening, say so.

**Nothing has been worked out for this month yet.**

Nothing has been built for that month. Step away and back with the arrows.

If something you saved does not appear, or a figure will not stop looking wrong, see "When something goes wrong".
