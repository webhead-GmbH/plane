# People and their terms

This page is for whoever sets people up: adding a colleague, saying how long their working week is, choosing how they are employed, keeping their leave and their rate, and marking them as having left. It also answers the question that brings most people here, which is why somebody's month has come out at a figure that makes no sense.

Everything on this page is on one screen, called **People**.

## Where this screen is, and who may use it

There is no link to this screen in the sidebar. The way in:

1. In the sidebar, open **My time**. You see your own month.
2. At the top right, open the **…** menu and choose **Everyone’s hours**. You are now on **Team time**, showing last month for everybody.
3. In the row of links beside the month name, choose **People**.

The breadcrumb at the top then reads **Team time** › **People**, and under the heading is the line "Who the company employs, and the terms each of them works under."

Only whoever looks after the team can change anything here. If you are not one of them and you open this address anyway, you are not turned away: you see the whole page with exactly one row, your own, and the figures on that row are not to be trusted. The **Hours a week** column will read **Not set up** even when your week is set, and the **Rates** window will say there is no rate even when there is one. Nothing you press will save. Your own month is on **My time** — see "My time — your own month".

## What the list shows

| Column           | What it holds                                                                          | Where it comes from                                                         |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Person**       | Their name, their email address below it, and sometimes a line about the CRM           | Their account here, not something typed on this screen                      |
| **Since**        | The day they started, written as `1 Mar 2024`, or a dash when nobody has said          | The **Started on** field                                                    |
| **Hours a week** | How long their week is, written as `38:30`, or the orange words **Not set up**         | The seven day boxes of their working week, added together                   |
| **Role**         | **Looks after the team** or **Employee**, and **has left** after it where that applies | The **Looks after the team** box, and whether they have been marked as left |
| **Action**       | A pencil, and a **…** menu                                                             | —                                                                           |

The list is in the order people were added, newest first. It is not alphabetical, there is no search and there is no way to sort it. With six to ten rows, read the whole list before deciding somebody is not there.

Under the table there is a line that explains why people who have gone are still listed: "Somebody who leaves is marked as having left rather than removed, so the months they worked stay readable."

Every duration on this screen is written as hours and minutes. `38:30` means thirty-eight hours and thirty minutes, not thirty-eight and a half written as a decimal. Both mean the same length; the screen writes it the first way.

### What **Not set up** means

**Not set up** in the **Hours a week** column means this person has no working week of their own. Almost always it means somebody was added and the next window was closed before it was saved.

While that is the case, nothing is expected of them: their **To work** is `0:00` every day, their **Balance** stays at `0:00`, and their month counts against nothing. It is not a small cosmetic gap. Put it right with the pencil, as described under [Their working week](#their-working-week).

If somebody has terms recorded but still no working week of their own, the company-wide standard week is used for them instead — `7:42` Monday to Friday, `38:30` a week. That only happens where the week was set outside this screen.

### The two lines about the CRM

Under somebody's email address you may see one of two lines. The CRM is the other system the office keeps; hours recorded here are copied over to the matching staff account there.

| The line on the row           | What it means                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CRM by email address only** | Nobody has said which CRM account this is, so the two systems are joined by the email address alone. Nothing has checked that an account with that address exists over there. |
| **No CRM account**            | Nothing at all joins this person to the CRM, so their hours never reach it.                                                                                                   |

No line at all means somebody has said which CRM account this is, which is the state to aim for. See [The CRM link](#the-crm-link).

## Adding somebody

The person must already have an account here. Accounts are made in Workspace settings, under Members; this screen cannot make one. If they do not appear in the list, that is why.

1. Press **Add someone**, top right. A window opens, titled **Add someone**, with the line "Pick the account whose hours this record will count."
2. Type part of a name or an address into **Search by name or email** if the list is long. It narrows as you type.
3. Click the person. The row is tinted and gets a tick.
4. Fill in **Started on**. It is empty and nothing fills it in for you — not even today's date. This is the day the company started employing them, and it goes on to decide where their first month begins, from when their terms are in force, and from when their starting balance applies. If you are entering somebody who has worked here for years, put in the real day they started.
5. Press **Add**. Until both a person and a date are given, **Add** is grey; resting on it says either "Pick somebody from the list first" or "Say when they started".
6. A green **Added** appears, and their own window opens by itself. Do not close it. Go straight on to [Their working week](#their-working-week) — a person with no working week has a month that counts nothing.

Two things you may see instead of a list of names:

- **Everybody already has an employment record.** — everyone with an account here is already on the list.
- **Nobody matches that.** — your search matched nobody. Clear the search box to see everybody again.

When the record is made, the timezone is set to `Europe/Vienna`. Change it in the next window if that is wrong for this person, as described under [Timezone](#timezone).

## Their working week

Press the pencil on somebody's row — it is named **Edit — Anna Berger** — and their window opens. The heading is their name, and under it: "Terms and a working week decide what this person's month comes to, so they are set together." The window has three parts: **The person**, **Terms** and **The working week**.

Pressing Escape, or clicking the dark area outside the window, closes it and throws away everything typed, without asking. There is no cross to close it with.

**The working week** is seven boxes, **Mon** to **Sun**. Under the heading: "How long each day should be. Type 7:42 for seven hours and 42 minutes, or 7.7 for the same length written as decimal hours. Leave a day blank when it is not worked."

| What you type            | What it is read as                                                                       |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `7:42`                   | 7 hours 42 minutes                                                                       |
| `7.7` or `7,7`           | the same 7 hours 42 minutes, written as decimal hours                                    |
| `462m` or `462min`       | 462 minutes, which is the same again                                                     |
| `7h` or `7`              | 7 hours exactly                                                                          |
| a blank box              | this day is not worked                                                                   |
| `7:75`, or anything else | refused: "One of the days is not a length of time. Type 7:42 or 7.7, or leave it blank." |

Leave a day blank rather than typing a zero into it. A blank and a zero mean the same thing to the system, and a blank is easier to read.

To the right of **The working week** a running total shows what you have typed so far, as **38:30 a week**. It changes with every keystroke. Use it to check yourself before saving.

At least one day must have a length in it. Saving with all seven empty is refused with "Fill in at least one day, so their month has something to be measured against. Leave the days they do not work blank."

An example. Anna works Monday to Thursday, seven hours and forty-two minutes a day. Type `7:42` into **Mon**, **Tue**, **Wed** and **Thu**, and leave **Fri**, **Sat** and **Sun** blank. The total beside the heading reads **30:48 a week**: four days of 7 hours 42 minutes is 4 × 462 minutes = 1,848 minutes, which is 30 hours and 48 minutes.

The working week is what four different figures are worked out from:

- how many hours are expected of them each day, which appears on their month as **To work**;
- what a public holiday is worth to them — their hours for that weekday, and nothing at all on a weekday they do not work;
- what a day of leave is worth to them;
- what "a day" means when leave is shown in days, which is the average of the days that have a figure in them. For Anna that is 7:42; for somebody working three long days and one short one it is the average of those four.

Changing somebody's week does not move any figure at that moment. This month and the one before it are worked out again by themselves, once an hour. Any other month is worked out again the next time somebody opens it. Months that have been closed are never worked out again at all.

## The four arrangements, and what each one records

Under **Terms** there is a box called **How they are employed**, with four choices:

| **How they are employed**          | Who it is for                                | What happens to the switch below |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| **On site, full time**             | An employee working a full week              | Switched on                      |
| **On site, part time**             | An employee working a shorter week           | Switched on                      |
| **Remote, full time, invoices us** | Somebody who invoices us for their own hours | Switched off                     |
| **Remote, part time, invoices us** | The same, on a shorter week                  | Switched off                     |

Choosing one of the four moves the switch under it for you. You can move it back by hand afterwards if this person is an exception; nothing forces the two to agree.

The choice itself changes nothing else. It is the switch under it that decides what is recorded.

### The switch

The switch is the box labelled **Expect set hours each day, keep a running balance, and count the leave they take**, with the note under it: "Leave this on for employees. Turn it off for anyone who invoices us for their own hours: no hours are expected of them each day, no balance is kept and no leave is counted."

This is the single most consequential thing on the screen. What each setting records:

|                                   | Switch on                                                                           | Switch off                                      |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **To work**, each day             | Their working week for that weekday                                                 | `0:00`, every day                               |
| **Balance**                       | **Worked** minus **To work**                                                        | `0:00`, always                                  |
| A public holiday                  | Adds their hours for that weekday to **Worked**                                     | Adds nothing                                    |
| A day of annual leave             | Adds their hours for that day to **Worked**, and comes off their leave for the year | Adds nothing, and takes nothing off their leave |
| Their leave figure on **My time** | Counted and shown                                                                   | Not counted                                     |
| Hours logged on work items        | Counted, either way                                                                 | Counted, either way                             |
| A rate and a monthly statement    | Unaffected, either way                                                              | Unaffected, either way                          |

So somebody who invoices us for their own hours shows a whole month of work with **To work** at `0:00` and **Balance** at `0:00`. That is correct and not a fault. It is also why granting the company a day off changes nothing for them — see "Public holidays".

Two things worth knowing about the switch being off:

- Recording a leave year for that person is still possible, and the figure will sit there being spent by nothing. If they are not owed hours by the day, they are not having leave counted either.
- A day recorded as sickness still adds their hours for that day to **Worked**, because sickness never comes out of anybody's leave.

Somebody who has been added but whose terms have never been saved is in the same position as somebody with the switch off: nothing is expected of them and their **Balance** is `0:00`.

## Changing somebody's terms later

Read this before changing anything for somebody who already has terms.

**In force from** is the day these terms start. For somebody being set up it is filled in with their start date, and you can leave it alone.

Recording a change from a later date — new hours from the first of next month, say — cannot be done on this screen today. If you put a new date into **In force from** and press **Save**, it is refused with:

> This overlaps a contract already recorded for this person. End the previous one first, so each day has exactly one set of terms.

There is no way to end the previous terms on any screen, so that message cannot be acted on.

The only save that goes through is one that leaves **In force from** exactly as it was. That rewrites the terms and the working week all the way back to that date. Every month that is still open, back to the day they started, is then worked out again against hours that were not in force at the time.

What to do:

1. Before touching anything, look at that person on **Team time** and see whether any of their months have been closed. Months that have been closed are protected: the save is refused with "A closed month was worked out against these terms. Reopen the month first if they genuinely need to change." Nothing is silently rewritten.
2. If nothing has been closed and nothing has been handed in, changing the week in place is harmless as long as you understand that it applies to the whole of their history here.
3. If a month in between has been handed in, agreed or closed, say so to whoever looks after the team before you change anything. Recording a change from a date is not something this screen can do yet, and saving over the old figures is not the same thing.

## Timezone

The box is labelled **Timezone, as Europe/Vienna**.

It decides which calendar day — and so which month — a person's hours fall on. A timer started at 23:40 on 31 March belongs to March, and this is the setting that says whose 31 March. It is the person's own zone that counts, not the zone of whoever is reading the screen.

Set it only for somebody whose working day is genuinely somewhere else. For everybody in Austria, leave it as `Europe/Vienna`.

The box accepts anything at all and never checks. A misspelling such as `Europe/Vienne` is saved without a word of complaint and then quietly behaves as `Europe/Vienna`. Type it carefully, in the form region slash city.

If the box is emptied, the workspace's own timezone is used, and failing that `Europe/Vienna`.

## The CRM link

**CRM staff id** says which staff account in the CRM this person's hours belong to. The box is empty by default and shows the hint "Leave empty to match by email".

Under the box, one of three lines tells you where things stand at the moment the window was opened. The line does not change as you type; it is what was true when you opened the window.

| The line under the box                                                                                                                                    | What it means                                          | What to do                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| "Set here, so the hours go to this account whatever the addresses say."                                                                                   | Somebody has said which account this is.               | Nothing.                                           |
| "Nobody has said which CRM account this is, so their hours are matched on the email address. Nothing checks that a CRM account with that address exists." | The two systems are joined by the email address alone. | Find their staff number in the CRM and type it in. |
| "Nothing joins this person to the CRM, so their hours never become a CRM timer."                                                                          | Nothing joins them at all.                             | Type their staff number in.                        |

The middle line is the one people misread. It does not mean a CRM account was found. It means nobody has said, so the address is being used as a guess, and nothing has checked the guess.

Only whole numbers are accepted. Anything else is refused with "A CRM staff id is a whole number. Leave it empty to match on the email address instead." Emptying the box puts them back on the email match.

## Looks after the team

The box labelled **Looks after the team — can see and change everyone's hours, absences and leave, in every workspace** does exactly what it says. It is not the same thing as administering a workspace, and it is not limited to one workspace: somebody with this box ticked can see and change every colleague's month everywhere.

The last one cannot be taken away. Un-ticking the box on the only remaining manager, or marking that person as having left, is refused with:

> This is the only person who looks after the team. Taking it away would leave nobody able to reach anyone's month, and nobody able to give it back. Make somebody else a manager first.

Tick the box for the new person and save that first, then make the change you wanted.

## Starting balance

Open the **…** menu on somebody's row and choose **Starting balance**. It answers the question "where did this person stand on the day we started counting". There is no history here to work it out from, which is why it is agreed with the person rather than calculated.

The figure of kind **Hours over or under** is the one the running balance chain hangs off. Their balance starts counting on the day it applies from, or on their start date when there is none, and anything dated before that day is never counted into the running balance, however carefully it was entered — see "Bringing hours in from somewhere else".

Before you record one, three things that cannot be taken back:

- There is no way to delete a starting balance. There is no button for it anywhere.
- Once the person has agreed it, it can no longer be corrected on this screen.
- **What for**, **Applies from** and **How sure** keep whatever you used last time. If you record an hours figure and then type a leave figure straight afterwards, the second one is filed as hours over or under as well, and cannot be removed. Check **What for** every time before pressing **Record it**.

To record one:

1. In **Balance, minus if they are behind**, type the figure. A leading minus means they are behind. Write it as `-12:30` for twelve hours and thirty minutes behind. Writing `12.30` does not mean twelve hours thirty: it is read as decimal hours and stored as twelve hours and eighteen minutes. There is nothing on the screen to read it back to you, so type the `:` form.
2. Pick **What for**: **Hours over or under**, **Leave**, or **Overtime to be paid**. Only **Hours over or under** feeds the running balance.
3. **Applies from** is filled in with their start date. This is the day counting begins for them.
4. Pick **How sure**: **Taken from records**, **Worked out from other records**, **Estimated**, or **From a conversation with them**.
5. **What it is based on** must be filled in, and the save is refused without it: "Say what this figure is based on. A number nobody can account for is one nobody can defend when the person it belongs to disagrees." Write where the figure came from, for example the old spreadsheet and the payslip it was checked against.
6. Press **Record it**. The list above reloads, a green **Recorded** appears, and the form empties ready for the next one.

Every figure then shows **Waiting for them to agree** in orange until the person themselves agrees it. Nobody can agree it on this screen, not even a manager looking at their own row. The person does it on **My time**, from the **…** menu, under **Starting balance** — and once they have, it cannot be taken back.

While a figure is not yet agreed, the pencil beside it, **Correct this figure**, replaces it: the old figure is kept and marked as replaced, and a new one is written in its place. **What for** and **Applies from** are greyed out while you correct, because a correction replaces one particular figure. Figures that have been replaced move into the small "1 replaced by a correction" line, which opens to show them.

## Rates

Open the **…** menu and choose **Rates**. This is what turns a closed month into an amount on a statement. Somebody with no rate gets a statement showing hours and no money: "No rate on record. A statement for this person will show hours and no amount."

Rates are readable only by whoever looks after the team, in both directions — nobody can see their own.

To add one: fill in **From**, choose **Paid** (**By the hour**, **A fixed amount each month**, or **A monthly amount plus overtime**), type the figure into **Per hour** or **Per month**, check **Currency**, and press **Add**.

Write the amount plainly. A comma is read as a decimal point, so a monthly salary typed the Austrian way as `3.500` or `3,500` is saved as three euros fifty, without a word of complaint. Write three and a half thousand as `3500` or `3500.00`.

A raise is not a change to the existing rate. Add a second rate starting on the day the raise takes effect; it takes over from that day, and the old one goes on pricing the months before it. The screen says the same: "Rates are kept by date, so a month already worked out keeps the rate it used."

After a raise, both rows read **From** and a date, with nothing marking which is in force. The one with the later date is the one in force. Do not delete the older one to make the list tidier. Deleting a rate is the one thing on this screen that changes what a closed month is worth, because a month is priced from whatever rate is on record at the moment somebody reads it. The confirmation says so:

> Every month is priced from the rate on record at the moment it is read, months already closed included. Deleting this changes what those months come to. Delete it only if it was recorded by mistake.

## Leave for the year

Open the **…** menu and choose **Leave**. This records how much leave somebody has; how leave is asked for and taken is on "Leave" and "Time away from work".

Leave is held in hours and minutes, not days, because a working day is not the same length for everybody. Days are worked out for display only, from the average of the days that have a figure in their working week.

The leave year runs from the anniversary of joining, not from January. Nothing fills the dates in for you, and a calendar year is the wrong guess. For somebody who started on 1 March, the current leave year is 1 March to 28 February.

To add one:

1. **Leave year from** and **Leave year to**. Both must be typed. Neither may be left blank, and the end may not fall before the start.
2. **Leave for the year (hours:minutes)**, for example `200:00`. Under the box a small line reads back what you typed, as **200:00 · about 26.0 days**. Check that line: it is the only thing that will tell you a figure was typed in the wrong unit.
3. **Carried over**, which may be left blank. A minus is allowed: "Optional. Use a minus if they have already taken some of it." This box has no read-back, so write it as `8:15` rather than `8.15`.
4. **What this is based on**, optional.
5. Press **Add**. A green **Leave year added** appears and the row joins the list above.

A new year that overlaps one already on the list is refused with "There is already a leave year covering these dates."

Keep the years unbroken. When the recorded years stop covering today, that person's line of leave left disappears from **My time** altogether, with no message anywhere saying why. Adding next year's row before the anniversary passes avoids it.

**Hold as final** freezes a row: "208:15 can no longer be changed on any screen afterwards. Check it against what the person was told before you hold it." A later change then has to be recorded as its own row with its own reason. Use it once the figure has been agreed with the person, not before.

## Marking somebody as having left

This does not delete anything. The person stays on the list, greyed, with **has left** beside their role, and every month they worked stays readable. They can be employed again later.

What it does do immediately: they can no longer open their own time page. From the moment you press it, **My time** fails for them, with no explanation on their screen. So finish their last month first — handed in, agreed and closed — before marking them as left. See "The month, from open to closed".

1. Open the **…** menu on their row and choose **Mark as left**, the red item at the bottom.
2. A confirmation opens, titled **Mark as left?**: "Anna Berger stops appearing in new months and can no longer open their own time page, so finish their last month before this. Everything already recorded stays as it is. You can employ them again later if they come back."
3. Press **Mark as left**. A green **Marked as having left** appears, and the row greys out.

They stop appearing in new months, and the hourly recalculation stops covering them.

Nothing on this screen records which day they left. If the leaving date has to be written down somewhere, it is not written down here.

To put somebody back, open the **…** menu on their greyed row and choose **Employ again**. There is no confirmation for this one: it takes effect at once, with a green **Employed again**, and the row goes back to normal.

## A month, with the sums

Daniel is an employee, **On site, full time**, `7:42` a day Monday to Friday, so **38:30 a week**. Take a month with 21 working days for him, containing one public holiday that falls on a Tuesday, and two days of annual leave that have been agreed.

Written in minutes, `7:42` is 462 minutes.

|                                       | Sum                                       | Comes to   |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| **To work**                           | 21 days × 7:42 = 21 × 462 = 9,702 minutes | **161:42** |
| Hours he records on the other 18 days | 18 × 7:42 = 18 × 462 = 8,316 minutes      | 138:36     |
| The public holiday adds               | his Tuesday, 462 minutes                  | 7:42       |
| The two days of leave add             | 2 × 462 = 924 minutes                     | 15:24      |
| **Worked**                            | 138:36 + 7:42 + 15:24                     | **161:42** |
| **Balance**                           | 161:42 − 161:42                           | **0:00**   |

Notice that the holiday and the leave are added to **Worked** rather than taken off **To work**. That is deliberate, and it is why a week off does not read as a week short. It is explained in full on "My time — your own month".

His leave for the year is `200:00`. A day for him is 7:42, so 12,000 ÷ 462 gives about 26.0 days. The two days he took are 924 minutes, so 12,000 − 924 = 11,076 minutes, which is `184:36`, or about 24.0 days left.

Now Sofia, in the same month. She is **Remote, part time, invoices us**, so the switch is off. Her week is Monday, Wednesday and Friday at `6:00`, which is **18:00 a week**.

|                                 | Sum                                          | Comes to  |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| **To work**                     | nothing is expected of her by the day        | **0:00**  |
| The public holiday adds         | nothing                                      | 0:00      |
| Two days of leave would add     | nothing, and take nothing off her leave      | 0:00      |
| Hours she records on work items | as logged                                    | 74:00     |
| **Worked**                      | 74:00                                        | **74:00** |
| **Balance**                     | nothing is owed, so nothing is over or under | **0:00**  |

Her statement for the month is 74.00 hours at her rate. At 65.00 EUR an hour, that is 4,810.00 EUR.

Two figures that look wrong on Sofia's row and are not: **To work** at `0:00`, and a public holiday that changed nothing for her. Do not put the holiday hours in by hand to make them match; she is not owed a public holiday.

## When a save is refused

A refusal appears as a red box titled **That did not go through** ( **That could not be saved** in the **Rates** and **Leave** windows). What you typed stays in the window, so you can correct it and try again.

| The message                                                                                                                        | What it means                                                             | What to do                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "This overlaps a contract already recorded for this person. End the previous one first, so each day has exactly one set of terms." | You changed **In force from** to a new date.                              | Put the date back to what it was. See [Changing somebody's terms later](#changing-somebodys-terms-later). |
| "A closed month was worked out against these terms. Reopen the month first if they genuinely need to change."                      | One of this person's months has been closed on the terms you are editing. | Nothing has been changed. Whether to reopen that month is a decision for whoever looks after the team.    |
| "A closed month was worked out against this schedule. Reopen the month first if it genuinely needs to change."                     | The same, for the working week.                                           | The same.                                                                                                 |
| "This is the only person who looks after the team…"                                                                                | You are removing the last manager, or marking them as left.               | Make somebody else a manager first.                                                                       |
| "You don't have the required permissions."                                                                                         | You are not one of the people who looks after the team.                   | Nothing on this screen will save for you.                                                                 |

Some of these sentences arrive in English even when the rest of the screen is in German. They are the same messages either way.

## If you get stuck

- For a person's own month, and what **To work**, **Worked** and **Balance** mean on it: "My time — your own month".
- For handing a month in, agreeing it and closing it, and for reopening one that has been closed: "The month, from open to closed".
- For everybody's months side by side, and for finding a person's closed months: "Team time — everybody's month".
- For asking for and agreeing time off: "Time away from work". For how much leave somebody has and what a day of it is worth: "Leave".
- For which days count as public holidays and what one is worth: "Public holidays".
- For entering months from before the system was in use, and for the order those months then have to be closed in: "Bringing hours in from somewhere else".
- For an error message not listed above: "When something goes wrong".

If the standard working week, the Austrian public holidays or the reasons for being away are missing on a new installation, that is a one-off setting-up step that has to be run once by whoever installed the system. Ask them; it cannot be done from any screen.

For anything else, ask whoever looks after the team. They are the people whose row on this screen reads **Looks after the team**.
